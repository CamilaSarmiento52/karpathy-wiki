# Elon Musk's management style
His account, from working with him, of how Elon Musk runs companies, a style he calls extremely unique and hard to describe even for people who have read about it; he says Elon runs the biggest startups ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @16:13).

## What he says
**Small, strong, technical teams.** Elon likes very small, strong, highly technical teams ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @16:13). Where teams at companies grow by default, Elon was a force against growth: he had to plead to hire, and had to fight to keep people, because Elon by default wanted to remove low performers ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @16:48). There is no non-technical middle management ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @16:48, @17:21).

**The vibe.** Elon wants the office to be a vibrant place, dislikes stagnation and large meetings, and encourages people to walk out of meetings where they are not contributing or learning ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @17:21, @17:53). There is much less pampering than at bigger companies; you are there to do your best technical work, with intensity ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @17:53).

**Connection to the team.** Unlike a CEO five layers up, Elon comes to the office and talks directly to the engineers, in meetings of about 50 people, rather than mostly to VPs and directors ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @17:53, @18:25). With a small strong team, the engineers and the code are the source of truth, so that is whom he talks to ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @18:57).

**The large hammer.** His example: if Elon hears twice that engineers lack GPUs, he calls the person in charge of the cluster, orders it doubled with daily updates, and when told Nvidia needs six months, asks to talk to Jensen; he removes bottlenecks ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @18:57, @19:29, @20:02). At a normal company afterwards, he says, you definitely miss aspects of this ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @20:02).

**Can founders copy it?** Asked by an audience member, he says it depends on the founder's DNA; if you make the culture clear when hiring, people are happy to go along with it, but changing to it later is very messy, so it works as long as you do it from the start and stay consistent ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @22:14, @22:46). He calls it a consistent model of company building with its own pros and cons ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @22:46).

The rowing-team joke that opens this part of the interview is the host's, retold from Elon; it is not his ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @15:06, @15:39).
