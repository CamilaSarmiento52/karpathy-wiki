# Transformer architecture
In his framing, all that happens in GPT is that a sequence of indices feeds into a Transformer and a probability distribution over the next index in the sequence comes out; the model is not complicated, and most of the implementation complexity is being clever with batching, across examples and over sequence length, for efficiency ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §intro).

## What he says
The minGPT README closes with his condensed notes on the OpenAI papers; the numbers below are his reading notes on those papers, not statements about his own models ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §References). On GPT-1 he records a 12-layer decoder-only Transformer with masked self-attention, 768-dimensional states, 12 heads, a 3,072-dimensional feed-forward layer, learned position embeddings, GELU, BPE with 40,000 merges, dropout 0.1 and about 117M parameters ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §References (GPT-1)). On GPT-2 he notes LayerNorm moved to the input of each sub-block plus one after the final block, residual weights scaled by 1/√N at initialisation, a vocabulary of 50,257, a context of 1,024, batch 512, and 48 layers with d_model 1,600 for about 1.542B parameters; he flags that he could not find the 1/√N residual initialisation in OpenAI's released GPT-2 code, only partly in image-gpt ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §References (GPT-2)). On GPT-3 he notes the same architecture as GPT-2 with alternating dense and locally banded sparse attention, a feed-forward width of four times d_model, a context of 2,048, Adam β2 = 0.95, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping at 1.0, linear warmup then cosine decay to 10%, a batch size ramped up during training, and a largest model of 96 layers, 96 heads, d_model 12,288 and 175B parameters ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §References (GPT-3)). On Image GPT he notes pixels in raster order with a 512-colour k-means palette, GPT-2 model code with Sparse-Transformer-style layer-dependent initialisation and zero-initialised logit projections, no dropout and no weight decay, with open questions of his own left in the notes ([[github-2023-01-08-mingpt-readme]] §References (Image GPT)).

**The block.** In the GPT lecture, the paper's feed-forward part is a simple per-position MLP; he adds it because after attention the tokens looked at each other but had no time to think on what they found ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:24:48, @1:25:20). Self-attention is the communication; the feed-forward, applied to each token independently, is where they think on the gathered data ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:25:52). A block intersperses communication (multi-head self-attention) with computation (feed-forward); the Transformer groups and replicates these blocks ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:26:26, @1:26:59). Stacking blocks sequentially without further ideas did not train well, which he attributes to deep-network optimisation issues, so he borrows residual connections and LayerNorm from the paper rather than tuning ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:27:31, @1:28:02). The feed-forward inner layer is four times the embedding dimension, following the paper's 512 to 2,048 ratio ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:31:23, @1:31:57).

**Residual connections.** Skip or residual connections come from the 2015 residual-learning paper and add transformed data back onto the previous features; he visualises a residual pathway from which computation forks off and projects back by addition ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:28:36, @1:29:11). Because addition distributes gradients equally to both branches, gradients hop through every addition node from the loss to the input, a gradient superhighway ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:29:11, @1:29:46). Residual blocks are initialised to contribute very little, so at init the pathway is almost unobstructed and blocks come online during optimisation ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:29:46, @1:30:19). Implemented as x = x + sa(x) and x = x + ffwd(x), with a linear projection back into the residual pathway after both ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:30:19, @1:30:51, @1:31:23). With residuals and the 4x inner layer, validation loss reaches about 2.08 and train loss starts to get ahead of validation, a little overfitting ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:31:57, @1:32:30).

**LayerNorm.** LayerNorm is very similar to batch norm, which made each neuron zero-mean unit-variance across the batch; changing the normalisation dimension from columns to rows turns his makemore BatchNorm1d into LayerNorm, normalising each example's vector ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:32:30, @1:33:04, @1:33:37, @1:34:10). Because the computation no longer spans examples, the running buffers and the train/test distinction go away; gamma and beta remain ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:34:43). Unlike the original paper's add-and-norm after the transformation, it is now common to apply LayerNorm before, the pre-norm formulation, which he implements as a slight deviation ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:35:19). Mean and variance are taken over the embedding dimension per token; batch and time act as batch dimensions ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:36:26). LayerNorm improves validation loss from about 2.08 to 2.06; he expects more help in bigger, deeper networks, and a final LayerNorm goes before the output linear layer ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:36:59).

**Decoder-only.** The result is a decoder-only Transformer with the major pieces of the original paper in place: no encoder and no cross-attention block, just self-attention and feed-forward ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:37:35, @1:42:35). It is decoder-only because text generation here is unconditioned, just imitating a dataset ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:43:07, @1:45:55). The original paper used encoder-decoder because it was a machine-translation paper, reading French tokens and decoding English between start and end tokens; the encoder runs a Transformer with no triangular mask so all tokens talk fully, and the decoder adds cross-attention where queries come from x but keys and values come from the encoder output ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:43:39, @1:44:12, @1:44:48, @1:45:22). This, he says, is exactly as done in GPT ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:45:55). The scaled lecture model has six layers, 384 embedding dimensions, six heads, block size 256, batch size 64 and dropout 0.2 ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:39:13, @1:39:46). GPT-3's architecture, he says, is nearly identical to what the lecture implemented, only far larger ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:50:56).
