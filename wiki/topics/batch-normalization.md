# Batch normalization
Batch normalization came out in 2015 from a team at Google and made training very deep nets reliable; the insight, as he puts it, is that if you want roughly Gaussian hidden states you can simply standardise them over the batch, because standardising is differentiable ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @40:52, @41:25, @41:58).

## What he says
He computes the mean and standard deviation over the batch dimension (shape 1×200) and standardises the pre-activations before tanh ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @42:33, @43:06, @44:13). Forcing the activations to stay unit Gaussian throughout training gives poor results, so the paper adds a learned scale (gain, initialised to 1) and shift (bias, initialised to 0) that backprop can move ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @45:19, @45:52, @46:27). With one hidden layer batchnorm gives the same 2.10, because the scale could already be set by hand; it pays off in deep nets with many layer types where tuning scales becomes intractable ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @48:06, @48:41, @49:16). It is customary to append a batchnorm layer after linear or convolutional layers to control the activation scale throughout the network ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @49:16, @49:49).

The cost is that examples in a batch become mathematically coupled in the forward and backward pass, so one example's hidden states jitter depending on the others ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @49:49, @50:23, @50:57, @51:31). This jitter acts as a regulariser, a kind of data augmentation, which is one reason it has been hard to replace ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @51:31, @52:04, @52:38). People have tried to move to layer, instance or group normalization, which do not couple examples ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @53:11, @53:44).

For inference the paper proposes calibrating the mean and std once over the training set after training; he implements this and gets the same loss while being able to forward a single example ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @54:16, @54:50, @55:22). Instead, a running mean and std are kept during training with a momentum update (0.999/0.001 here), outside gradient descent and under `torch.no_grad`, and they come out close to the calibrated values ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @55:54, @57:35, @58:09, @59:48, @1:00:26). Epsilon (default 1e-5) prevents division by zero when the batch variance is zero ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:00:58). A bias in the layer before batchnorm is subtracted out, gets zero gradient and is wasteful; the batchnorm bias takes over, which is why ResNet's convolutions use `bias=False` ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:01:31, @1:02:04, @1:02:37, @1:07:40, @1:08:13). His summary: the parameters are gain and bias, trained by backprop; the buffers are running mean and std, updated by a running average; the layer standardises over the batch, then scales and shifts ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:03:09, @1:03:44, @1:04:19). The ResNet bottleneck block repeats the motif weight layer, normalization, nonlinearity (conv, batchnorm, ReLU) ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:06:34, @1:07:07).

PyTorch's `BatchNorm1d` takes `num_features`, `eps`, `momentum` (default 0.1), `affine` and `track_running_stats`; with batch size 32 a momentum of 0.1 may thrash and 0.001 is safer ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:11:34, @1:12:06, @1:12:39, @1:13:13). His own module keeps a `training` flag like PyTorch, uses batch statistics when training and running statistics otherwise, and follows the paper in tracking the variance rather than the std ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:21:35, @1:22:08, @1:23:15). He places batchnorm after the linear layer and before the nonlinearity, says placing it after the nonlinearity gives similar results, and that it can also go before the loss, in which case gamma rather than the weight makes the softmax less confident ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:46:30, @1:47:04). With batchnorm the activations are homogeneous regardless of the linear gain, even without fan-in scaling, but the update scale changes so the learning rate may need retuning, here by about 10× ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:47:37, @1:48:44, @1:49:18, @1:49:50, @1:50:24, @1:51:00). Training a batchnorm version with his learning-rate finder gave very similar results, which he reads as performance no longer being bottlenecked by optimisation ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:53:45).

In the following lecture, writing batchnorm's backward pass by hand, he normalises the variance by 1/(n-1), Bessel's correction, rather than the paper's 1/m, deliberately ([[youtube-2022-10-11-makemore-4-backprop-ninja]] @1:00:11, @1:00:43, @1:05:16). He says the paper uses the biased 1/n estimate during training but the unbiased n-1 estimate when calibrating running statistics for inference, which he finds extremely confusing; 1/n almost always underestimates the variance, which matters with small mini-batches ([[youtube-2022-10-11-makemore-4-backprop-ninja]] @1:05:49, @1:06:22, @1:06:57). `torch.var` takes an `unbiased` flag whose default he believes is true but the docs do not state; the `BatchNorm1d` docs say biased but the implementation uses the unbiased version for the running std, which others have flagged in issues ([[youtube-2022-10-11-makemore-4-backprop-ninja]] @1:06:57, @1:07:31). BatchNorm takes no keyword to choose the estimator consistently in train and test, so in his view anyone using it has a bit of a bug, less of a problem with larger batch sizes ([[youtube-2022-10-11-makemore-4-backprop-ninja]] @1:08:05, @1:08:38). He derives the layer's whole backward pass analytically on paper, backpropagating in the order x-hat, sigma-squared, mu, x, and lands on one line of code that uses only dL/dy and the x-hats from the forward pass, which he calls the notable exception to the rest of the backward pass being about three lines per layer ([[youtube-2022-10-11-makemore-4-backprop-ninja]] @1:38:18, @1:39:25, @1:48:17, @1:53:52).

In the WaveNet lecture he recaps the layer as one that keeps running mean and variance trained outside backpropagation by exponential moving average, has a training flag because its behaviour differs between train and eval, and couples the activations of examples in a batch; he calls it a weird layer with state, and state is harmful, that causes a lot of bugs because you must set the right mode and wait for the statistics to settle ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @03:18, @03:52, @04:25). Sampling with BatchNorm left in training mode on a single example gave NaN, because the variance of one number is not a number; he calls this a bug introduced by not maintaining training state ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @15:30, @16:03, @16:35). On a 3-D input of (32, 4, 68) his `BatchNorm1d` reduced only over dimension 0, so it silently kept a running mean of shape (1, 4, 68), maintaining separate statistics for each of the four positions instead of 68 channels ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @38:53, @39:26, @40:32). `torch.mean` accepts a tuple of dimensions, so reducing over (0, 1) gives 68 channel statistics; he makes the reduce dims depend on `x.ndim` and raises for anything else ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @41:42, @42:13, @42:49, @43:22). PyTorch's `BatchNorm1d` expects (N, C) or (N, C, L) and reduces over dims 0 and 2, whereas his version assumes channels are last, (N, L, C), and reduces over 0 and 1; he acknowledges the deviation, says he prefers it that way, and keeps it ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @43:22, @43:55, @44:28). The fix moved validation loss from 2.029 to 2.022, which he says may not be statistically significant but is expected because each statistic is now estimated from 32×4 numbers rather than 32 ([[youtube-2022-11-21-makemore-5-wavenet]] @45:01, @45:35).
