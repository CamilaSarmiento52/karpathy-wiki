# AI for education
How he wants AI to scale a really good teacher: the teacher designs the course and the AI is the front end that takes each student through it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:21, @32:54). His planned course LLM101n is being developed by Eureka Labs, according to its README ([[github-2024-08-01-llm101n-readme]] §intro).

## What he says
**The LLM101n README (2024-08-01).** The banner says the course does not yet exist, is currently being developed by Eureka Labs, and that the repo is archived until it is ready; it links Eureka Labs' site and says nothing else about it ([[github-2024-08-01-llm101n-readme]] §intro). The course would take learners from basics to a ChatGPT-like web app with minimal computer science prerequisites ([[github-2024-08-01-llm101n-readme]] §intro).

**Why education (No Priors, 2024-09-05).** He says he has always been an educator and loves learning and teaching ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @30:13). He sees most AI activity as replacing or displacing people; he is more interested in what empowers people, calls himself "team human", and wants people empowered and more amazing than today rather than sidelined by automation ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @30:44, @31:18). He asks how far a person could go with the perfect tutor for every subject and thinks AI can approach that ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @31:18). Asked about the primer in The Diamond Age, a host's reference, he says he is inspired by aspects of it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @31:50).

**One course, scaled.** In practice he is building a single course, the one you would go to if you want to learn AI; he taught CS231n at Stanford, which he calls the first deep learning class, and the question now is how to scale such a course to perhaps 8 billion people in many languages and at all levels ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @31:50, @32:21). The teacher-on-the-back-end design is a separate claim ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:54). Translation is super low-hanging fruit because current models are really good at it; adapting to a person's background with analogies to what they know is not low-hanging but not far, though it needs work to actually work rather than demo ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @34:31, @35:03, @35:35). He says he hopes AI can destroy gatekeeping by scarce resources such as lab lineage ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @36:40). Communities give education but also culture, what is prized, which strongly shapes motivation; in Toronto, he says, starting companies did not occur to students, who talked about internships at a fixed set of companies ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @37:13, @37:46, @38:19). He thinks culture may be the dominant variable, because the education itself is already the easier part, with a ton available ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @38:51).

**Audience and timing.** The first course is roughly undergraduate level in a technical area, for any age, and he expects the school-then-work model to break down as people return to school more often ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @41:01, @41:34). He had hoped for late that year but, with distractions, expected early next year, in his words at recording ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @42:08). A host, not he, names "Eureka" when asking about the course ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @41:01).

**What children should study.** Asked what little kids should study, he says mostly math, physics and CS kinds of disciplines, which he calls the best core of thinking skills; physics classes shaped how he thinks, though he notes his own background ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @42:08, @42:41). Pre-AGI this is useful, and post-AGI you still want empowered humans ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @42:41). The critical period, when people have a lot of time and attention, should go mostly to manipulation-heavy rather than memory-heavy work; he is not opposed to other disciplines, but thinks about 80% should be like this ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @43:14).

**Eureka as a Starfleet Academy (Dwarkesh, published 2025-10-17).** Asked why he is not at an AI lab, he says he feels some determinism about what labs do and doubts he would uniquely improve it; his big fear is humanity disempowered on the side, as in WALL-E or Idiocracy, and he wants humans well off, which he thinks is achieved through education ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:57:26, @1:58:04). He describes Eureka as building the Starfleet Academy: an elite, up-to-date institution for technical knowledge ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:58:04, @1:58:37). He thinks education will fundamentally change with AI, but the obvious prompting-based approaches are early ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:59:11). His bar is a one-on-one Korean tutor; at current capability that tutor cannot be built, a separate claim ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:59:51, @2:01:07).

He is building the first course, LLM101n, with nanochat as its capstone, and then intermediates and a small team of TAs ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:02:21, @2:03:02). To him education is a hard technical process of building ramps to knowledge, measured in "eurekas per second", another claim ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:03:02, @2:03:38). He collaborates with AI to make the material and feels empowered compared with building CS231n, but is very much in the loop: the models are not yet at the point of creatively making the content ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:04:55, @2:05:33). He has in mind a physical institution, and a tier below it a digital offering ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:07:24). He expects a lot of re-education as jobs change ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:08:04). How he learns and teaches is the method [[learn-by-building-and-explaining]] ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:17:33).
