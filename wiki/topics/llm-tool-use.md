# LLM tool use
Giving an LLM tools such as calculators, code interpreters and search, as people lean on tools for what they are bad at, and filling its context window with retrieved information ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @31:41, @32:15, @32:48).

## What he says
**Tools (State of GPT, 2023).** Since models may not know what they don't know, he suggests telling them in the prompt that they are not very good at mental arithmetic and spelling out how to call the calculator ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @32:15, @32:48). In ReAct-style prompting the model's actions in its thought–action–observation rollout can include tool use ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @28:54, @29:28).

**Retrieval.** He says the pendulum swung from retrieval-only systems to memory-only LLMs, and the retrieval-augmented space in between works extremely well in practice ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @32:48). The context window is the Transformer's working memory, and loading it with information relevant to the task makes it work extremely well ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @32:48, @33:21). The emerging recipe is to chunk documents, embed them, store the vectors, and at test time query and stuff relevant chunks into the prompt; LlamaIndex is his data-connector example ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @33:21, @33:53). He likens it to people looking things up in a textbook or documentation rather than relying on memory ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @33:53, @34:27).

**Tools in ChatGPT (Intro to LLMs, November 2023).** Finetuning teaches ChatGPT that some queries call for tools rather than a direct answer; it emits special words that trigger, for example, a search, and the results are fed back as text ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @27:43, @28:18, @28:49). His demo on Scale AI's funding rounds uses the browser to find them, a calculator to impute missing valuations because, like people, the model is not good at math in its head, Python with matplotlib to plot, and DALL-E to make an image ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @28:49, @29:53, @30:25, @31:00, @32:40). Tool use ties existing computing infrastructure together with words; it is not just working in its head and sampling words, and people solve problems with tools in the same way ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @32:07, @33:12). The valuation extrapolation in that demo is ChatGPT's fitted trend line, which he congratulates the team on in jest, not his estimate ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @31:00, @31:34).

**Multimodality and customization.** He calls multimodality a major axis: seeing images, as when Greg Brockman's pencil sketch of a joke website is turned into working HTML and JavaScript, generating them, and hearing and speaking in speech-to-speech mode ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @33:12, @33:45, @34:19). Customization is another: OpenAI's GPTs app store allows custom instructions and uploaded files, which ChatGPT references through retrieval-augmented generation, like browsing your files, and future customization might include finetuning ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @40:27, @41:00, @41:32). The aim is many expert models for specific tasks rather than one model for everything ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @42:06).

**The cognitive core (No Priors, 2024-09-05).** He describes a small cognitive core as a thing that thinks and, when it needs information, knows how to use tools to look it up; see [[a-cognitive-core-could-be-a-billion-parameters-or-less]] ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @27:27).

**Search and code (Deep Dive, 2025-02-05).** Web search works by the model emitting special tokens around a query; the sampling program pauses, runs the search and pastes the results into the context window ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:33:53, @1:34:26, @1:34:58). The model learns when and how to search from a few thousand example conversations, and already understands what a good query is from pretraining ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:36:04, @1:36:36). He calls search an additional mitigation for hallucination and factuality; see [[llm-hallucinations]] ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:39:25). He would not trust a model's mental arithmetic and asks it to "use code", trusting a Python interpreter's result much more ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:55:32, @1:56:08, @1:56:42, @1:57:14). Counting dots works with code because copying tokens into a Python string is easy for the model, and the interpreter does the counting ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:59:57, @2:00:33).

**The tools in his apps (How I use LLMs, 2025-02-27).** Without tools, he says, the model is a self-contained zip file, with no calculator, Python interpreter or web browsing ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @11:48). Asking it directly is fine when the knowledge is common and not recent ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @13:25). For anything decided after training, such as a TV season's release date, it needs search: it emits a special token, the app stops sampling, runs the query and pastes the pages' text into the context window ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @32:24, @32:57, @33:31, @34:04). Some models decide to search by themselves and sometimes you are better off telling them to; in his tests in the video Claude and Gemini 2.0 Pro lacked search ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @35:44, @36:50, @37:56, @38:29). He goes to Perplexity, out of habit, whenever the answer would be in the top links of a search: recent or changing information, esoteric facts that may have changed, or the gist of something trending ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @39:03, @39:36, @40:12, @40:45, @41:17, @41:52).

Deep research combines search and thinking over tens of minutes and returns something like a custom research paper with citations; he found ChatGPT's the most thorough and Perplexity's and Grok's shorter ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @42:26, @46:44, @47:53). File uploads put documents such as a PDF into the context window ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @51:13, @52:50). With the Python interpreter the model writes a program, special tokens make the app run it, and the result comes back as text; OpenAI taught ChatGPT by example when to use it, so 30 × 9 is answered from memory and a large multiplication with code ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @59:02, @1:00:07, @1:00:40, @1:01:16, @1:01:51). Advanced Data Analysis acts like a junior data analyst whose code you must read; how he checks it is [[verify-citations]] ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:04:39, @1:05:46). ChatGPT's memory is a database of text strings about the user prepended to every conversation, which he says he is coming around to, and custom instructions change its behaviour globally; he told it to be educational and set a Korean formality level ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:55:35, @1:56:08, @1:56:39, @1:57:12, @1:57:44). These availability details are as of the video ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @36:50).

In his nanochat letter-counting task the trained answer tries a manual count and then double-checks it with the Python interpreter tool, written as `<<'pollocks'.count('o')=2>>` in his example ([[github-2025-10-24-nanochat-strawberry-guide]] §intro, §Reasoning structure). Because the synthetic data is clean, the two always agree in training ([[github-2025-10-24-nanochat-strawberry-guide]] §Reasoning structure).

## Multimodality
**2023.** In Intro to LLMs (2023-11-23) he called multimodality a major axis: seeing and generating images and hearing and speaking; see the section above ([[youtube-2023-11-23-intro-to-llms]] @33:12, @34:19).

**2025.** In the Deep Dive he says audio, as spectrogram slices, and images, as patches, can be tokenized and interleaved with text in one model, so multimodality is not a fundamental change ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:10:20, @3:10:53). He expects models to become natively multimodal, agents that run for minutes or hours under human supervision, and computer use such as OpenAI's Operator, which takes keyboard and mouse actions on your behalf ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:09:48, @3:11:26, @3:12:00, @3:12:33, @3:13:05).

**Fake audio and native audio (How I use LLMs, 2025-02-27).** What he calls "fake audio" pipes speech through speech-to-text and text-to-speech; advanced voice mode handles audio tokens natively in the model ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:27:32, @1:28:40, @1:29:14). His own dictation habit is [[voice-input]] ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:23:04). The voice-mode demonstrations in the video are AI-generated voices and are not quoted here ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:29:47). Image generation in ChatGPT at the time, DALL-E 3, wrote a caption that a separate image model drew, stitched together rather than native ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:48:16, @1:48:50). In his summary, models that handle modalities natively are more powerful than ones that tack on a separate model through text ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @2:09:23, @2:09:58).
