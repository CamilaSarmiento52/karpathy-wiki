# Weight initialization
How the weights are scaled before training decides whether the network starts out confidently wrong, whether its activations saturate, and whether gradients survive a deep stack; he wants roughly unit-Gaussian activations throughout the net and gets there by dividing weights by the square root of their fan-in ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @05:38, @29:48, @30:21).

## What he says
He says you almost always have a rough idea of the loss to expect at initialisation from the loss function and the problem setup ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @04:28). With 27 possible characters and no reason to prefer any, the expected initial loss is -log(1/27), about 3.29, not the 27 his network recorded ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @05:03, @05:38). The high loss comes from logits taking extreme values so that the network is confidently wrong; he wants the logits roughly zero, or at least equal, at initialisation ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @05:38, @07:51, @08:25). He zeroes the output bias and scales the output weights by 0.01 rather than exactly zero, keeping a little entropy for symmetry breaking ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @09:33, @10:39, @11:11). He prefers not to set weights to exactly zero and says he is nervous about doing so, though for this output layer he thinks it would be fine ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @10:39). For the hidden layer he sometimes multiplies biases by 0.01 for a little diversity and finds in practice that can help optimisation a little ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @24:15). Validation loss moved from 2.17 to 2.13 by fixing the softmax, then to 2.10 by fixing tanh saturation ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @25:57).

Multiplying a unit-Gaussian input by Gaussian weights expands the standard deviation, to about 3 with fan-in 10, and he wants roughly unit-Gaussian activations throughout the net ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @29:15, @29:48). Dividing the weights by the square root of fan-in preserves unit standard deviation ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @30:21, @30:58). He points to the paper "Delving deep into rectifiers" (He et al.), which studies ReLU in conv nets and adds a factor of √2 because ReLU discards half the distribution ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @31:30, @32:04, @32:36). The paper finds that initialising the forward pass well also roughly initialises the backward pass, and that the fan-in versus fan-out choice does not matter much ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @33:10, @33:45, @34:18). PyTorch's `kaiming_normal` takes a mode and a nonlinearity, with gain 1 for linear, √2 for ReLU and 5/3 for tanh; he says this is in his opinion probably the most common initialisation now ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @33:45, @34:50). The gain compensates for tanh and ReLU being contractive, squeezing the distribution ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @35:23). In practice he just normalises weights by the square root of fan-in; the exact version is std = gain / √fan_in ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @37:00). For his W1 the fan-in is 30 (embedding 10 times block size 3), giving a scale of about 0.3 versus the 0.2 he found by eye, and the result is the same 2.10 without magic numbers ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @38:43, @39:16, @39:47, @40:19). PyTorch's `nn.Linear` defaults to a uniform initialisation in ±1/√fan_in with gain 1 ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:09:55, @1:10:28).

A one-hidden-layer MLP is forgiving of bad initialisation, but with 50-layer networks the problems stack up and a network can fail to train at all ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @27:02, @27:35). In his deep linear-tanh sandwich, gain 1 shrinks the activations towards zero over depth and gain 3 saturates them, and the gradients distort when the gain is too small or too large ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:28:47, @1:29:56, @1:31:03, @1:31:35). He does not know where PyTorch's 5/3 gain for tanh comes from; he searched briefly and found nothing, but empirically it stabilises the sandwich ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:29:56, @1:30:30). In a sandwich of linear layers alone the right gain is 1; with 5/3 the activations diffuse and the gradients shrink with depth, an asymmetry he calls bad for deep nets ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:32:09, @1:32:43, @1:33:16). Before normalization layers, Adam and residual connections, training deep nets was a balancing act and very deep networks were basically impossible to train ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:33:49, @1:34:21). Calibrating gains in a linear-tanh sandwich, he says, feels like balancing a pencil on your finger ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:45:56). With batch normalization the activations come out homogeneous regardless of the linear gain, even without fan-in scaling, though the update scale changes so the learning rate may need retuning ([[youtube-2022-10-04-makemore-3-activations-batchnorm]] @1:47:37, @1:48:44, @1:49:50).

The ng-video-lecture README restates the point for the GPT lecture's code: he did not go much into model initialisation in that lecture, but says it is quite important for good performance; the code trains and works fine, yet converges more slowly because it starts off in a not-great spot in weight space ([[github-2023-01-31-ng-video-lecture-readme]] §intro). The proper initialisation, he says, is nanoGPT's `_init_weights` function in `model.py`, which cannot be copy-pasted into the lecture code because the modules are named and stored differently; he planned a supplementary lecture and a code push to follow ([[github-2023-01-31-ng-video-lecture-readme]] §intro).
