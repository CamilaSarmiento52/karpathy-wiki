# LLM evaluation
How he judges a model he has trained against someone else's: in the llm.c GPT-2 (124M) reproduction he plots validation loss on held-out FineWeb and accuracy on HellaSwag, a benchmark he says is commonly used to assess LLM capability, and compares both with OpenAI's released checkpoint ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro).

## What he says
Evaluation enters llm.c as a missing piece: on 2024-05-03 he lists "evaluation (WikiText 103?)" among the components still needed for a proper GPT-2 reproduction ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Functional)). On 2024-05-24 the checklist for a serious "production" pretraining run includes evaluating on a standard dataset, then HellaSwag ([[github-2024-05-24-llmc-pytorch-cross-checks]] §intro).

**Validation loss is not a fair comparison.** His run beats OpenAI's checkpoint on the withheld FineWeb validation set, but he says this is not the ideal metric: GPT-2 was trained on the never-released WebText, and the internet's statistics may have been different five years earlier ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro).

**HellaSwag as the main number.** So he also plots HellaSwag accuracy, which he calls nice, smooth and well-behaved; he would mostly look at HellaSwag and treat FineWeb validation as a confirmation ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro). He adds a caveat himself: HellaSwag has no math or code, so it slightly favours his common-crawl-like training data ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro). The `-h 1` flag runs it, and he chose it as something comparable across papers ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Args guide)).

**Reference points.** His run reaches 29.9 (his result) against 29.4 for OpenAI's GPT-2 (124M) checkpoint and 33.7 for GPT-3 Small (124M) as cited in the GPT-3 paper's Appendix H (comparison numbers); he reminds the reader that his model trained on 10B tokens while the GPT-3 models all trained on 300B ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro).

**HellaSwag in the GPT-2 lecture (2024-06-09).** HellaSwag (2019) is a sentence-completion multiple-choice set: one natural continuation and wrong options drawn from ActivityNet and WikiHow contexts ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:28:13, @3:28:47, @3:29:22, @3:29:55). Per the paper, the wrong options were generated adversarially by language models to be hard for models but easy for humans, who scored 95% against 48% for the best models of the time ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:30:32). Today models score about 96%, so he considers it crushed, the last points likely dataset errors ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:31:05). People like it, he says, because it is smooth and gives early signal: small models start at the 25% chance level and improve slowly; it appears in the GPT-3 paper, not GPT-2's ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:31:05, @3:31:39, @3:32:15).

Small models cannot do multiple choice with labels, so he scores each option as a token completion and picks the one with the lowest average loss, which he believes is how GPT-3 did it; seeing all options at once is an easier format ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:32:47, @3:33:55, @3:34:29, @3:35:02). His script scores GPT-2 124M at 29.55% and GPT-2 XL at about 49%; the Eleuther harness gets slightly different numbers, possibly because it uses the multiple-choice format ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:37:16, @3:37:48). HellaSwag might have leaked into FineWeb's training data; he hopes Hugging Face deduplicated against it but cannot be sure, so it is not a perfect eval ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:45:57, @3:46:29). Saving checkpoints lets you evaluate more carefully later, for example with the Eleuther harness on math, code or other languages ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:54:47, @3:55:21).

**Wider evals, and a suspected overlap (2024-06-12).** After the 774M run beat expectations he plans to convert the checkpoint to Hugging Face format and run a wider, bigger suite of evals before the next big run ([[github-2024-06-12-llmc-gpt-2-774m]] §intro (Reflections)). A downward kink in validation loss exactly at the one-epoch boundary makes him nervous that FineWeb's validation and training sets overlap more than expected ([[github-2024-06-12-llmc-gpt-2-774m]] §intro (Reflections)).

**Reading the 1.6B evals (2024-07-11).** OpenAI's GPT-2 scores 2.83 on FineWeb-Edu validation loss by his measurement, and his run passes it by about step 5,000; he calls that unfair because GPT-2 trained on the never-released WebText, and shows that finetuning OpenAI's model for 1,000 steps at LR 1e-4 drops its loss to 2.61 as it adapts to the new data ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Evals)). He looks at validation loss as a sanity check and wants fixed third-party evals for the real comparison; HellaSwag, simple common-sense scenarios where the model picks the right continuation, is well-behaved, smooth, common, often cited and gives early signal ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Evals)). Even HellaSwag is not an ideal single comparison, he says: it tests simple English and common sense, not multilingual, math or code, which WebText may have weighted more, and good evals are harder at GPT-2's capability because such models do not understand multiple choice and their samples cannot dent math or code evals ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Evals)). Exporting to Hugging Face format enables the Eleuther harness and Hugging Face sampling, and `dev/eval` has instructions for the Eleuther harness and the Open LLM Leaderboard evals ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Model export)).

**Leaderboards (Deep Dive, 2025-02-05).** LM Arena ranks models by blind human comparisons; he says it was good for a long time but he thinks it has become a little gamed in the last few months and trusts it less, pointing to Claude Sonnet's low rank against how widely it is used, so he treats it as a first pass ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:15:18, @3:16:57). As of 2025-02-05 Google led it with DeepSeek third ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:15:51, @3:16:24). To keep up he recommends the AI News newsletter by swyx and friends and following people you trust on X ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:17:32, @3:18:04).

**CORE, bits per byte and ChatCORE (nanochat launch post, 2025-10-13).** During pretraining nanochat evaluates the CORE metric from the DCLM paper, which he describes as a normalised, broad measure of autocompletion over 22 datasets including HellaSwag, ARC, PIQA and Winograd ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Pretraining). It reports validation bits per byte, which normalises each token's loss by its byte count; he says "Bits per byte is a much better measure than just the typical cross-entropy loss" because it is tokenizer-invariant and comparable across vocabulary sizes ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Pretraining). His d20 base model reaches about 0.81 bpb and CORE 0.22 (measured), against GPT-2 large at 0.21 and GPT-2 xl at 0.26 (comparison) ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Pretraining).

For the chat model he uses ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge and MMLU, multiple choice with about 25% chance, and GSM8K and HumanEval, with a 0% baseline (comparison) ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining). ChatCORE, his attempt to extend CORE to chat models, subtracts each task's baseline so random is 0 and averages, a single-number summary ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining). After midtraining ChatCORE is 0.0730, after SFT 0.0884, which he calls a small bump (measured) ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining, §Supervised Finetuning (SFT)); after RL, GSM8K is 0.0758 (measured) ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Report card). He says "The evals are still fairly incomplete" ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining).

**Small models must be taught multiple choice.** At this scale, he says, the model does not learn multiple choice from random internet data: it must learn to associate choices with letters and emit the right one, so midtraining mixes in 100K MMLU auxiliary_train questions (setting) because common evals like MMLU are multiple choice ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining). "Multiple choice is pretty hard for models this small." ([[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining). **Note:** the 2024-07-11 llm.c post says models at GPT-2's capability do not understand multiple choice, which makes good evals harder; here he teaches it in midtraining ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Evals); [[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Midtraining).

**d34 against d32 (2025-11-20).** The d34 base model reaches CORE 0.3382 and validation bpb 0.7045 (measured), against d32 at 0.3168, the d20 speedrun at 0.22 and GPT-2 at 0.25 (comparison) ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro). From midtraining to SFT, d34 goes from 0.5333 to 0.5435 on ARC-Challenge, 0.7071 to 0.7365 on ARC-Easy, 0.1137 to 0.1296 on GSM8K, 0.1159 to 0.0488 on HumanEval, 0.4297 to 0.4311 on MMLU and 0.4087 to 0.4100 on ChatCORE (measured) ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro). His d32 comparison table ends SFT at ChatCORE 0.2734, with an RL column only for GSM8K, 0.1994 (comparison) ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro). "So our ChatCORE lifted quite a lot, from 0.27 -> 0.41." ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro). HumanEval "stubbornly plunged" during SFT, which he attributes mostly to noise and expects tuning could recover; he expected more lift on GSM8K, where midtraining helps and SFT "botches it a bit again", possibly not tuned super well ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro). **Note:** GPT-2's CORE is 0.25 here and 0.26 for GPT-2 xl in the launch post; these are given as stated, not reconciled ([[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro; [[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Pretraining).

**CORE, not validation loss (miniseries v1, 2026-01-07).** He did not want to compare models by validation loss, which he calls simple but subtle and deceiving, and says "Only actual metrics are real and comparable."; see [[only-actual-metrics-are-real-and-comparable]] ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §intro (Comparison to GPT-2/GPT-3 miniseries)). He uses the DCLM paper's CORE, an ensemble over 22 datasets (comparison); DCLM's code for it was "complicated and bloated", so he stripped it into one simple, dependency-free file, `core_eval.py` ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §intro (Comparison to GPT-2/GPT-3 miniseries)). For GPT-2 he calculated CORE by running the released models: Small 0.114, Medium 0.185, Large 0.215, XL 0.257 (measured by him on OpenAI's models) ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (GPT-2 / GPT-3 CORE scores)). GPT-3 was never released, so he found six tasks in both CORE and the GPT-3 paper's tables, calibrated a simple mapping on GPT-2 and estimated Small 0.148, Medium 0.216, Large 0.266, XL 0.291, 2.7B 0.329, 6.7B 0.361, 13B 0.385 and 175B 0.427 (estimates), saying the solid evals and well-aligned points give him confidence they are not far off ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (GPT-2 / GPT-3 CORE scores)). He attributes GPT-3's edge at equal size to data, architecture and optimisation improvements and to far more tokens, 300B against GPT-2's estimated ~100B; all scores are CORE v1, not v2 ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (GPT-2 / GPT-3 CORE scores)). The miniseries' own results run from d10 at 0.071 CORE and 1.0312 val bpb in 5 minutes to d20 at 0.1708 and 0.8572 in 59.5 minutes (measured), with d12 at 0.1059 and d16 at 0.1332 ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (Miniseries v1 CORE scores)). **Note:** GPT-2 XL's CORE is 0.257 here, 0.25 for GPT-2 in the d34 post and 0.26 for GPT-2 xl in the launch post; likely rounding of one figure, given as stated ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (GPT-2 / GPT-3 CORE scores); [[github-2025-11-20-nanochat-d34-model]] §intro; [[github-2025-10-13-nanochat-discussion-introducing]] §Pretraining).

**The time-to-GPT-2 target (2026-01-31).** The leaderboard's target is the DCLM CORE metric, "a comprehensive evaluation across 22 high-quality benchmarks"; GPT-2's CORE score is 0.256525 (comparison) ([[github-2026-01-31-nanochat-beating-gpt-2]] §The Goal). It differs from modded-nanogpt's leaderboard in targeting CORE rather than validation loss ([[github-2026-01-31-nanochat-beating-gpt-2]] §The Goal). The record d24 reaches CORE 0.25851 (measured) ([[github-2026-01-31-nanochat-beating-gpt-2]] §Reproduce). **Note:** GPT-2's CORE is 0.256525 here, 0.257 for GPT-2 XL in the 2026-01-07 post; rounding of one figure, given as stated ([[github-2026-01-31-nanochat-beating-gpt-2]] §The Goal; [[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §Details (GPT-2 / GPT-3 CORE scores)).

**A suspicion of goodharting (X post, 2026-03-05).** Because NVIDIA's ClimbMix data helped nanochat so much out of the box, he writes "I am slightly suspicious about about goodharting", though reading the paper it seems about okay to him; the post does not name the metric ([[x-2026-03-05-nanochat-gpt-2-in-two-hours]]). **Note:** on 2026-01-07 he worried about changes that game validation loss and preferred CORE; see [[only-actual-metrics-are-real-and-comparable]]. Here the worry is about data, there about a metric, and neither post refers to the other ([[github-2026-01-07-nanochat-miniseries-v1]] §intro (Comparison to GPT-2/GPT-3 miniseries); [[x-2026-03-05-nanochat-gpt-2-in-two-hours]]).
