# Self-driving
The field he spent maybe five years in, and the one he draws many of his analogies to AGI from, which he says may be just because he is familiar with it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @00:36).

## What he says
**A product, a decade after the demo (No Priors, 2024-09-05).** He feels self-driving has reached AGI "a little bit", because there are systems you can pay to ride, such as Waymo in San Francisco, which he has taken many times ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @00:36, @01:07). His first Waymo ride was a demo from a friend who worked there, about a decade earlier, "2014 or so" in his words; it was basically a perfect drive, and it took ten years to go from that demo to a city-scale product he can pay for ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:07). Asked whether the delay was regulatory or technical, he says a 30-minute demo does not show everything they had to deal with for a decade, so there is a massive gap between demo and product, and a lot of it is also regulatory ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39). Globalisation has not happened at all: the world has not changed yet, that will take a long time, and he suspects AGI will look similar ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39, @02:11).

**Tesla and Waymo.** People think Waymo is ahead; he personally thinks Tesla is, because Tesla has a software problem and Waymo a hardware problem, software problems are much easier, and Tesla already has cars deployed at scale ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @02:11). He drove the latest build the day before and says it did some miraculous driving; he expects Tesla to be ahead in scale and revenue in ten years ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @02:45, @03:17). Tesla does use expensive sensors such as lidar, but at training time, on cars that map; that is distilled into a vision-only test-time package, an arbitrage on sensor cost he calls brilliant and underappreciated ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @03:49). He thinks it will work because the pixels have the information and the network will be able to use it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @03:49, @04:21).

**The net eats the stack.** The plan at Tesla, he says, was always for the neural net to eat through the stack: from single-image detection to multiple images to predictions over time, discarding C++ code until it outputs steering commands ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @04:21, @04:54). His understanding, which he hedges because they do not talk about it, is that Waymo tried this but did not do it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @04:54). He suspects that in about ten years Tesla's system will be just a neural net, video in and commands out ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @05:28). The intermediate predictions did not mislead development, in his view: imitating humans end to end gives too few bits of supervision for billions of parameters, so the intermediate representations develop the features and detectors; he suspects, without being on the team, a lot of pretraining before end-to-end finetuning ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @05:28, @06:02).

**Humanoid robots.** A host notes that he worked on the Tesla humanoid before leaving; he says basically everything transfers from cars: cars are robots, and Tesla is a robotics-at-scale company ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @06:02, @06:37). Early Optimus had the same computer and cameras and ran the car networks, trying to recognise drivable space in the office, and with some finetuning it generalised a little ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @07:11). The in-house expertise, CAD models, supply chain, labeling team and approach, was reconfigured for the robot ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @07:43, @08:15). Where it should start is a separate claim ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @08:48). On the humanoid form, he says people underappreciate the fixed cost of any one platform, a humanoid is easy for people to teleoperate, the world is designed for humans, and data collected for one task benefits the others through transfer learning, as with a single multitask language model ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @10:26, @10:59, @11:33). Cheaper wheeled platforms make sense, but he wonders if they lead to a local minimum ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @12:06). He does not claim a good window into what is missing: the lower body is inverted-pendulum control where he doubts imitation learning, the upper body needs teleoperation and data collection, and he expects teleoperation first and people supervising robots, all gradually ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @13:13, @13:45, @14:17). He knows of no single technical impediment; the rest is data, training, deployment and grunt work ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @14:17).

**The YC talk (2025-06-19).** He retells the Waymo demo as a perfect drive in 2013, and says that twelve years later self-driving still needs teleoperation and humans in the loop ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @26:01, @26:35, @27:09). **Note:** the two talks give different years for the same demo, "2014 or so" and 2013; both are his own recollections and neither is corrected here ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:07; [[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @26:01).

**Not done yet (Dwarkesh, published 2025-10-17).** Asked why self-driving took a decade, he pushes back that it is not even near done ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:43:43). The demo-to-product gap, he says, is a march of nines, each nine the same amount of work; see [[there-is-a-massive-gap-between-demo-and-product]] ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:45:36, @1:46:16). He is not fully convinced by Dwarkesh's suggestion that LLMs give the hard representation parts for free: models are still fallible with gaps, and generalisation is not magical out of the box ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:48:44, @1:49:18). Deployments are minimal and uneconomical, and he says there are elaborate teleoperation centres with people in the loop some of the time, though he does not know the full extent; in some sense the person has been moved somewhere you cannot see them ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:49:18, @1:49:54, @1:50:32). He loves Waymo and takes it all the time, and thinks Tesla's approach is the more scalable one ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:51:07, @1:51:42). He does not want to call it something that took a decade, because the start was in the 1980s and the end, self-driving at scale with no need for driver's licences, is not near ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:51:42). Society, law, insurance and people putting cones on Waymos, he says, will have equivalents for AI ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:54:13). He says he does not know Waymo's stack and that his guesses about it are made up, so they are not recorded here ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:51:07).

**Note:** the YC talk tells the same story, the Autopilot network growing while C++ was deleted, and says Software 3.0 is now eating through the stack the same way; see [[software-3-0]] ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @04:56, @05:32; [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @04:21).
