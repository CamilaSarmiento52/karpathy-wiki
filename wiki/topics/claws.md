# Claws
The word he uses for a layer above coding agents that takes persistence much further: something that keeps looping, that you are not interactively in the middle of, with its own sandbox, that does things on your behalf even when you are not looking, and that may have more sophisticated memory than an agent's default compaction when the context runs out ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @07:07). The captions write "Claude" where he says "claw", and "Open Claude" for OpenClaw ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @07:07).

## What he says
**Going up the stack.** Asked what mastery of coding agents looks like, he says everyone is going up the stack: from a single agent session to multiple agents collaborating in teams, and then claws ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @06:35, @07:07). The layers stack like an onion: LLMs, then agents, then claw-like entities, then multiples of them, instructions to them and optimisation over the instructions, each taken for granted in turn ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @22:23, @22:57). Mastering this, he says, is mostly a skill issue; see [[agent-failures-mostly-feel-like-a-skill-issue]] ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @22:57).

**OpenClaw.** He credits Peter (the caption says "Peter Steinberg") with innovating in about five ways at once in OpenClaw and putting them together: a crafted personality document, the memory system, having fun with it, and one WhatsApp portal to all the automation ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @07:40, @08:12, @08:44). He says personality matters a lot and many other tools do not appreciate it ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @08:44). The comparison of personalities at @08:12, which sets Claude beside Codex, may mean Anthropic's Claude or a claw, given the caption error, and is not used here ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @08:12).

**Dobby, his home claw.** In January, he says, he went through a period of "claw psychosis" and built a claw that takes care of his home, which he calls Dobby the elf claw ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @09:16). He told it he thought he had Sonos at home; it scanned the local network, found the system with no password protection, found the API endpoints through web searches and played music in the study, in about three prompts ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @09:16, @09:49, @10:20). It did the same for the lights and now controls his lights, HVAC, shades, pool, spa and security system; a vision model (the caption says "Quinn", probably Qwen) looks at camera changes and texts him on WhatsApp, for example when a FedEx truck pulls up ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @10:20, @10:54). He used to use six different apps for the home and no longer does: "Dobby controls everything in natural language." ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @11:26).

**Why it resonates.** He thinks claws match what people expect an AI to be, a persona that they can tell things and that remembers, an entity behind the WhatsApp, while a raw LLM is a token generator too primitive to "type check as AI" for most people ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @11:59, @12:34). He expects home automation like this to become free and table stakes in a year or two or three, even for open models, with a claw handling the details while you are not involved ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @14:13, @14:47).

**Caution.** He has not given his claw email, calendar or full access to his digital life, because it is still very new and rough around the edges, and from caution about security and privacy ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @15:53). **Note:** his general account of attacks on LLMs is on [[llm-security]]; this interview does not discuss specific attacks ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @15:53).
