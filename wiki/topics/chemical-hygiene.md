# Chemical hygiene
His name for the household choices he makes to limit exposure to chemicals and plastics in water, air, food, fabrics and personal care, written up as "my (evolving, opinionated) guide to chemical hygiene" ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (opening)). He says he is not a professional toxicologist or food scientist and calls the guide a work in progress; everything on this page is his personal practice and opinion as of 2025-12-18, not medical or scientific fact ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (closing)).

## What he says
**The grocery store (2025-07-08).** In a single X post he describes the ideal grocery store as selling only minimally processed food (he specifies NOVA Group 1), no "edible food-like substances", organic, local and fresh; food should not be more complex than this, he says, yet he does not believe such a store exists ([[x-2025-07-08-ideal-grocery-store]]). The 2025-12-18 post links this very post as an example of what a grocery store should look like ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food sourcing)).

**The guide (2025-12-18).** He calls it a follow-up to his digital hygiene post, written up because he keeps ranting about the topic to his friends ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (opening)). He frames it as going after the 80:20 low-hanging fruit ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Dental hygiene)).

**Water.** He calls water the easiest section, with concrete, high-confidence recommendations that in his experience fewer than 5% of his friends have adopted ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)). He recommends that all drinking water come from reverse osmosis with a remineralisation post-filter, ideally under the sink, and says Brita and other basic filters are not good enough in his view; he also recommends a whole-home sediment-and-carbon filter, or at least a shower filter for renters ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)). He advises against bottled water, saying you cannot control that supply chain, especially light and heat during delivery ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)). He advises against tap water, which he says is a lot less clean than people think, and with proper dental care calls its "public health" additives unnecessary and possibly mildly harmful, "especially and controversially fluoride" ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)). He links a study he calls an "Example fun study" on Parkinson's risk near golf courses; the finding is the study's ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)). He suggests hiring a local company to install the filters, handle the roughly yearly filter changes and test the improvement ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water)).

**Air.** He uses HVAC filters and/or a standalone purifier: he got a Dyson Big+Quiet and names an IQAir model as the top tier ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Air)). He advises avoiding combustion at home: candles (beeswax only if you really like them, which he occasionally burns), gas stoves in favour of induction, and unsealed gas fireplaces ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Air)). He advises skipping humidifiers unless the air is very dry, and all air fresheners, oil diffusers and fragrances, which he calls a very poorly regulated wild west of synthetic chemicals ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Air)). He measures basic home air quality with a device he bought and suggests a professional test panel, for example for radon and mold ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Air)).

**Food.** "Food is the hardest category to control", he says, because of deep supply chains he says have been ruthlessly efficiency-maxxed over decades with little to no regard for public health externalities ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food)). His sourcing choices: organic produce; Pacific, wild-caught salmon rather than farmed; "pasture raised" and organic eggs and dairy, calling "cage free" and "free range" scams; pasture-raised, organic, air-chilled chicken ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food sourcing)). For packaged goods he looks for a short ingredient list that makes sense and uses the BobbyApproved and Ivy scanner apps; he says not to buy "edible food-like substances" and to shop only at the walls of the supermarket ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food sourcing)). He also advises avoiding canned soups and touching receipts, and currently uses a delivery service, Locale ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food sourcing)).

**Cooking.** "Rule number 1: avoid plastics, specially in combination with heat." ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Cooking & preparation)). He uses stainless steel or cast-iron pans, glass, steel or ceramic storage, wooden boards and utensils, and avoids hot food or drinks through plastic: delivery containers, disposable cups, cheap coffee machines and tea bags ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Cooking & preparation)). On cooking oil he says "Seed oils are currently hotly contested.", that he personally finds them highly suspicious, and that he prefers olive oil, avocado oil, butter, ghee or tallow ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Cooking & preparation)).

**Fabrics and cleaning.** He prefers natural fibres (cotton, linen, hemp, wool, silk) because, he says, synthetic fibres shed micro- and nano-plastics carrying plasticizers; he warns that much "bamboo" fabric is chemically processed rayon or viscose, citing a 2010 FTC case ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Fabrics)). For cleaning supplies he looks for few, simple ingredients, fragrance- and dye-free, and currently uses Blueland ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Cleaning supplies)).

**Where he has not changed.** Dental hygiene is a category where he says he could not make a dent: he still uses a plastic-bristle toothbrush and plastic floss, finding the plastic-free ones less comfortable or harder to use, and is experimenting with water floss ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Dental hygiene)). On toothpaste he says it seems very trendy to diss fluoride, but "I'm not personally convinced just yet and I still use a fluoride toothpaste." ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Dental hygiene)).

**Fluoride, both sides.** In the water section he calls fluoride in tap water unnecessary and possibly mildly harmful given proper dental care, with "controversially" attached; in the dental section he is not convinced against fluoride "just yet" and uses fluoride toothpaste ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water), (Dental hygiene)). The first is about drinking water, the second about toothpaste; he does not reconcile them, and the wiki does not either ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Water), (Dental hygiene)).

**Sunscreen and wellness.** He prefers mineral sunscreen, citing concerns over some chemical-sunscreen ingredients as possible endocrine disruptors, says he is a little suspicious of the need for and overuse of sunscreen, and applies it only for prolonged, intense, high-UV exposure ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Sunscreen)). "I am much less well-versed in cosmetics more generally", he says, because he does not use them ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Sunscreen)). Under wellness he lists Zone 2 cardio, saying most people spend too much time in Zone 3+; sauna, linking a Bryan Johnson Blueprint post for the claim that it reduces toxins via sweat; and vitamin D, saying you are probably deficient, with a full blood panel about yearly ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Wellness)).

**Limits.** Even following all of it, he says, "you are simply decreasing risk, you can never eliminate it"; his examples are plasticlist.org's testing, in which Boba Guys was the worst offender at the time, and a farm whose cows ate their hay's plastic wrap ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Learn more)). He calls the industry's cost-driven hyper-optimization a deep rabbit hole beyond the post, naming modern wheat and modern honey, and says the post's goal is met if a few people start paying attention ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Learn more)). He now uses his Instagram for non-AI, lifestyle topics and recommends four books, on plastics and food ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Learn more)).

**His TLDR.** "Keep your home unsophisticated.": filter water and air, eat real food with few, sensible ingredients and simple supply chains, say no to dyes and fragrances, and avoid plastics, especially handled, heated or frozen ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (TLDR)). He invites readers to ask their favourite LLM about any item and to send him their thoughts, and says he will update the guide ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (closing)).
