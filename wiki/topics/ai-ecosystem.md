# AI ecosystem
How he wants the AI industry to look: not a few megacorps but a healthy, thriving ecosystem, which he pictures as a coral reef of startups in all the nooks and crannies of the economy ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @21:10).

## What he says
Asked what gives him meaning, he says he cares not about any one specific company but a lot more about the ecosystem, which he wants healthy and thriving, a boiling soup of cool stuff ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @21:10). By default he is more hesitant about five megacorps taking over, worried because AGI is such a magnifier of power, and says he has to think that through more ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @21:41). His parting message to founders is a question: how to keep a vibrant ecosystem of startups that continue to win against big tech ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @36:06).

**Open weights, open source, proprietary.** He extends the operating-system analogy to the market: an oligopoly of proprietary systems like Windows and macOS, plus Linux with an infinity of distributions, which he thinks LLMs may come to resemble ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:27). He says we have to be careful with naming: models such as Llama and Mistral are not, in his view, open source; receiving them is like being tossed a binary of an operating system, useful but not fully ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:27, @07:59). He names a few fully open-source LLMs, LLM360 among them (the caption garbles the other names), that release the whole infrastructure needed to train the model from its data ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:59). With only the weights you can finetune, but the more you finetune the more the model regresses on everything else; adding a capability without regression wants a mixture of the old and new data, which needs the training loop and the dataset ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @08:32). He proposes three categories, open-weights, open-source and proprietary models, and expects it to look much like today's operating-system ecosystems ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @09:05).

**Will open models keep pace?** He says he does not really know, because the models are so capital-intensive ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @30:33). He reasons that Meta can afford to train at scale while its money printer is unrelated, which gives it an incentive to release models to empower the ecosystem and borrow its best ideas ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @30:33, @31:06). So far, he says, Meta has only released open weights and should go further, perhaps by constraining itself to data sources that are easy to use; he credits it for publishing a paper and a logbook but says it could do much better at fostering the ecosystem ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @31:06, @31:40).

**What would make it more vibrant.** He points to what is available, citing his own "build the thing, build the ramp" post: many people build things and far fewer build ramps so that people can understand them ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @32:13). He wants people to be more open about what they learned, how they trained, and what worked and did not, and sees quite a bit of momentum in the open ecosystems already ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @32:45).

**Closed platforms and an open fallback (No Priors, 2024-09-05).** Asked by a host about market structure, he describes the state of the ecosystem as an oligopoly of a few closed platforms and an open platform somewhat behind, such as Meta's Llama, mirroring the open-source software ecosystem ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @25:13, @25:45). Thinking of AI as an exocortex, he borrows a crypto saying and asks whether "not your weights, not your brain" holds ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @25:45). The unlabelled captions mix his lines with a host's on ownership and renting a better brain, so those are not attributed to him here ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @25:45, @26:19). He sees a trade-off, and suggests people may by default use closed models because they are amazing while keeping a fallback, as people already fall back to open models they fully control when closed APIs go down ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @26:19). When a host says it is quite important that open-source models keep progressing, he answers that he agrees 100%, and that this is not an obvious point people agree on ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @26:53). **Note:** compatible with his Sequoia answer that he does not know whether open models will keep pace; here he says it matters that they do ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @30:33; [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @26:53).
