# Prompting LLMs
In-context learning, in his framing, is a Transformer learning from examples placed in its prompt, in its activations and without gradient descent, which he says is kind of what makes Transformers really special ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @55:30, @56:34). In State of GPT (May 2023) he says prompting is to a large extent making up for the cognitive difference between brains and LLMs ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @23:55, @24:27).

## What he says
**In-context learning (CS25, 2023).** He points to the GPT-3 paper, *Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*, which he would have renamed something like "Transformers are capable of in context learning or like meta learning" ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @55:30). In the paper's setting the prompt holds a passage plus example question–answer pairs, and accuracy improves with more examples in context ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @56:02, @56:34). He reads this as the Transformer learning in its activations, unlike finetuning, which updates on example–answer pairs by gradient descent ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @56:34). The paper distinguishes an outer loop, training by gradient descent, from an inner loop, in-context learning as the model reads the sequence ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @56:34, @57:07). He reports that recent papers hint something like gradient-based learning may happen inside the activations; that is the papers' position, and his own reason it is not impossible, which he calls much more hand-wavy, is that forward, backward and update add to the weights like a ResNet, and a Transformer is a ResNet ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @57:07, @57:38).

On extending memory, he likes keeping the context fixed and teaching the model, via prompt examples, to write to a scratchpad between special tokens that external logic saves and lets it attend over; because it is so meta-learned it can be taught such gadgets, like a person learning to use a notepad ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @1:08:47, @1:09:20).

**Prompting around LLM psychology (State of GPT, 2023).** He describes LLMs as token simulators: they do not know what they don't know or what they are good at, do not reflect, sanity-check or correct mistakes along the way, and have no separate inner monologue ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @22:45, @23:22). Their advantages are a very large fact-based knowledge in tens of billions of parameters and a relatively large, perfect working memory: whatever fits in the context window is losslessly available through self-attention ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @23:22, @23:55). Prompting, he says, is largely making up for this difference ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @23:55, @24:27).

So he spreads reasoning over tokens with few-shot examples that show their work or by asking for step-by-step thinking ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @24:59, @25:32). Self-consistency samples several times and keeps the good ones or takes a majority vote, because a model that samples a bad token cannot recover and continues down the blind alley ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @25:32, @26:05). Bigger models such as GPT-4 often know when they failed, for instance a poem that rhymes when asked not to, but only check if prompted to ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @26:40, @27:12). He groups such techniques as recreating System 2, citing Tree of Thought, and describes ReAct's thought–action–observation rollouts with tool use ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @27:12, @27:46, @28:54, @29:28). Constrained prompting, his example being Microsoft's Guidance, clamps token probabilities to force a template such as JSON ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @34:27, @35:00).

**His default recommendations.** He splits the work into achieving top performance first and optimising cost second ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @37:12). For the first: start with GPT-4, the most capable model by far; write very detailed prompts, as for a contractor who cannot email back, while catering to LLM psychology; retrieve and add relevant context; show with few-shot examples rather than only tell; offload hard tasks to tools and plugins; and think in chains, reflection and multiple samples rather than one prompt ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @37:12, @37:44, @38:17). Only after prompting is squeezed should you consider finetuning, with RLHF a fragile research zone; then cut cost with lower-capacity models or shorter prompts ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @38:17, @38:51). **Note:** the full order, with the Sequoia interview's distillation step, is the method [[make-it-work-then-make-it-cheap]] ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @37:12).

**Example code in context (CUDA MODE keynote, published 2024-10-03).** He expects few-shot learning to help an LLM write low-level code: prompted to write GPT-2 in C from scratch you probably would not get llm.c, but with llm.c in the context you are a lot more likely to ([[talk-2024-10-03-cuda-mode-keynote]] @22:37).

**Base models and pasted context (Deep Dive, 2025-02-05).** A base model can be prompted to elicit knowledge, for example by starting a top-10 list of Paris landmarks ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @50:34). Few-shot prompts such as English–Korean word pairs use in-context learning, so apps can be built on a base model alone ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @56:04, @56:37, @57:11). An assistant can be instantiated from a base model by a prompt shaped like a web page of human–assistant turns, though it then also hallucinates the next human turn ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @57:11, @57:43, @58:17, @58:52). Recall works better if you paste the material into the prompt, such as the text of the first chapter of Pride and Prejudice, than if you rely on the model's memory ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:40:32, @1:41:04). Saying "don't use any tools" is enough to make ChatGPT answer from memory ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @1:38:15). For hard math or code he reaches for thinking models; as of 2025-02-05 about 80–90% of his own use was GPT-4o, since thinking for 30 seconds about a factual question is overkill ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @2:40:27).

**Custom GPTs as saved few-shot prompts (How I use LLMs, 2025-02-27).** A custom GPT, he says, is just a saved prompt: nothing special happens beyond prompting, and it saves retyping a prompt you reuse ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:59:23, @2:00:31, @2:01:06). He gives a description plus concrete examples, a few-shot rather than a zero-shot prompt, and says he finds this always increases accuracy ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:59:58, @2:00:31). For longer examples he wraps each in XML-like tags so the model sees where it begins and ends, and compares it to teaching a person, where showing by example works much better than explaining ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @2:03:17, @2:03:49). His examples are Korean-learning GPTs: a vocabulary extractor whose lines go into Anki, a detailed translator, and one that reads, translates and breaks down screenshots of subtitles ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:58:48, @2:02:11, @2:04:21, @2:04:55).

**Test-time adaptation (Animals vs Ghosts, 2025-10-01).** In the post's Appendix he says Dwarkesh briefly argued on the podcast that LLMs do learn continually at test time, in context rather than in the weights, and that he thinks Sutton did not fully react to it; the point is Dwarkesh's ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §Appendix). His own view: "In context learning is a form of test time adaptation", and it is why few-shot prompting works ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §Appendix). **Note:** this fits the CS25 reading above that the Transformer learns in its activations, unlike finetuning ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @56:34; [[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §Appendix).

**Funky gradient descent (Dwarkesh, published 2025-10-17).** The statement that in-context learning is not gradient descent is Dwarkesh's; he pushes back that it is pattern completion in a token window and that it is possible it runs a small gradient-descent loop internally, recalling a paper on in-context linear regression whose weights showed analogies to gradient descent ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @14:58, @15:34, @16:10). He hedges: nobody knows how in-context learning works, but it is probably doing some funky gradient descent internally ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @16:46, @17:24). The context window as working memory, against the weights as hazy recollection, is on [[pretraining-is-lossy-compression-of-the-internet]] ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @18:39).
