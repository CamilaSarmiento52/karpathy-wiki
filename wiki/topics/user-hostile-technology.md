# User-hostile technology
His name for technology drifting toward the "complex, dependency-bloated, user-hostile" products he contrasts with the pocket calculator, which is self-contained, owned outright and private ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro).

## What he says
**The calculator (2024-09-08).** He says he loves the calculator not as a device you would buy today but as a product of technology and a symbol of a kind of philosophy ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro). Operated with your fingers it becomes a plugin for your brain that extends its arithmetic and makes you smarter ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro). It has an almost zero "dependency footprint" on the rest of the technosphere: it needs only light for its solar panel and/or batteries, a universal commodity; you buy it in one exchange for money and from then on it is yours; carried back thousands of years, it would just work ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro).

**What it does not do.** He lists what modern technology asks and the calculator does not: no internet connection to set up, no bluetooth permissions or precise location, no account or login, no updates every other week, no subscription upsells, no attempt to become a platform, no card on file, no usage tracking, no review prompts, no harvesting or leaking of your information, no newsletter, no Terms of Service notifications, and no breaking when the servers go down ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro). The computation you perform is private, secure, confined to the device, and logged nowhere; "You paid for it and now it is yours", and it has no other master ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro).

**The counter-example.** A CO2 monitor with thousands of positive reviews demanded that he create an account, download an app and allow precise location before it would report the CO2 in his room ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro).

**Why the drift? (posed as questions).** He offers an analogy, companies as solving the problem of maximising shareholder value under the constraints of regulation, and then asks rather than asserts whether the drift is companies hyperoptimizing that objective, why sell a product when you can rent it and monetise customer data, and whether governments are lagging and out-gunned ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro). As consumers, he says, we can be more aware of the drift and fight back by eating into the shareholder-value term; as developers we can add an extra term to the optimisation, "the regularizing gradient of ideology" ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro). He links four related works by others that he found enjoyable and influential, among them the Internet of shit account; their arguments are theirs, not his ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro).

**The fraud apparatus (Digital hygiene, 2025-03-17).** He describes tech companies as incentivised to build comprehensive profiles of you, to monetise for advertising or to sell to data brokers who enrich, de-anonymise and resell them ([[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro). Regular data breaches gather your information into archives that feed an underground industry of hacks, phishing, ransomware, card fraud and identity theft ([[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro). He calls smart devices incredibly insecure internet-connected computers that gather data, get hacked often and send data back for analytics, and says to avoid them; his example is a CO2 monitor from China, bought in his younger and naive years, that demanded to know everything about him and his precise location before it would report the CO2 in his room, and he links the same Internet of shit account as the calculator post ([[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro (Internet of Things)). **Note:** the calculator post's CO2 monitor demanded an account, an app and precise location; he does not say the two are the same device ([[blog-2024-09-08-i-love-calculator]] §intro; [[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro (Internet of Things)).

He wants to live a secure digital life with products that leak only the necessary information, and wishes to pay for software so that incentives are aligned ([[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro (closing)). The practices he follows are [[digital-hygiene-checklist]]; he admits still trading sovereignty for convenience with Gmail and X ([[blog-2025-03-17-digital-hygiene]] §intro (What's not on the list)).
