# Self-attention
The problem self-attention solves is gathering information from the past in a data-dependent way, for example a vowel looking for consonants in its past; every token emits a query (what am I looking for) and a key (what do I contain), and the affinities are the dot products of a token's query with all keys ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:03:36, @1:04:09). He calls the derivation probably the most important part of the video to understand ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:01:54).

## What he says
**The aggregation trick.** Before writing the attention block he introduces a mathematical trick at the heart of an efficient self-attention implementation ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @42:09). The fifth token should only communicate with tokens one to four, because information must flow from the past and never from the future it is trying to predict ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @43:17). The simplest communication is to average the channels of the current and all preceding tokens into a feature vector that summarises the token in its history, which he calls an extremely weak, lossy form of interaction that discards spatial arrangement, to be fixed later ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @43:52, @44:26). A lower-triangular matrix of ones (`torch.tril`) multiplied with a matrix sums a variable number of its rows; normalising the rows to sum to one turns the sums into incremental averages ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @47:14, @48:26, @50:05, @50:39). PyTorch broadcasts a T-by-T weight matrix across the batch as a batched matrix multiply, giving the same result as the for loop ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @52:20, @52:52). This is a weighted aggregation whose weights sit in a triangular T-by-T array ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @53:59). The third form starts weights at zero, masked-fills the upper triangle with negative infinity and applies softmax, which produces the same matrix; he prefers it because the zeros can be read as affinities, how much of each past token to aggregate, and the minus infinity says the future cannot communicate ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @54:33, @55:07, @56:14, @56:46). Long story short: weighted aggregation of past elements is a matrix multiply with a lower-triangular matrix whose entries say how much of each element fuses into a position ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @57:53).

**Queries, keys and values.** Aligned keys and queries interact strongly, so the token learns more about that specific token than others ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:04:45). Keys and queries are produced in parallel and independently by linear layers with no bias; communication happens only in the query-key matmul, which transposes the last two dimensions to give a B,T,T affinity tensor ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:05:18, @1:05:52, @1:06:25). The weights are now data-dependent and differ per batch element ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:06:58, @1:07:31). A vowel at the eighth position might emit a query for consonants at positions up to four, and a matching key channel gives a high dot product ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:07:31, @1:08:04). Raw affinities range over negative and positive values; masking the upper triangle and exponentiating and normalising gives a distribution summing to one ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:09:11, @1:09:45). A value vector is also produced; the output aggregates values, not the raw x, so the head output has head-size dimensions ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:10:20, @1:10:53). He frames x as private information, the query as what I am interested in, the key as what I have, and the value as what I will communicate if you find me interesting ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:10:53). Elements across the batch never talk to each other; with batch four and block size eight there are four separate pools of eight nodes ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:13:38). It is self-attention because keys, queries and values all come from the same source x; attention in general is broader, and in cross-attention the queries come from x while keys and values come from a separate source such as an encoder ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:15:55, @1:16:28). The head module registers `tril` as a buffer, not a parameter, and crops the generation context to block size because of the positional table ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:19:15, @1:20:55). After adding one head with a lowered learning rate, validation loss falls from about 2.5 to 2.4 ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:21:27).

**No notion of space.** Nodes do not know where they are, which is why positional encodings are added; this differs from convolution, where filters act over a spatial layout ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:12:32, @1:13:03).

**Scaling.** The paper divides the query-key dot product by the square root of head size, calling it scaled attention ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:17:01). With unit-Gaussian k and q the naive weights have variance on the order of head size (16 here); scaling restores variance one ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:17:34). This matters because the weights feed softmax, which should be fairly diffuse especially at initialisation; softmax converges towards one-hot vectors when its inputs take extreme values, as he shows by multiplying a tensor by eight ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:17:34, @1:18:10, @1:18:42). A too-peaky softmax means every node aggregates from a single other node, which is not wanted at initialisation; the scaling just controls variance at init ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:18:42).

**Causal masking.** Future tokens must not communicate with past ones in language modelling, because they would give away the answer ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:14:11, @1:15:19). For tasks like sentiment analysis you may want all tokens to talk fully; that is an encoder block, which deletes the masking line, while what he implements is sometimes called a decoder block because of its autoregressive format with the triangular mask ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:14:11, @1:14:45). Both are allowed; attention does not care and supports arbitrary connectivity ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:15:19). Later he says what makes the model a decoder is the triangular mask, which gives the autoregressive property that lets you sample from it ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:43:07).

**Multiple heads.** Multi-head attention is just multiple attentions applied in parallel with their results concatenated over the channel dimension ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:22:01, @1:22:35). Instead of one 32-dimensional channel he runs four 8-dimensional heads whose outputs concatenate back to 32, which he compares to group convolution ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:22:35, @1:23:08). Four heads bring validation loss to about 2.28 from 2.4 ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:23:41). Multiple communication channels help because tokens have many different things to look for, such as consonants, vowels or particular positions ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:23:41, @1:24:15). The head count is like a group size: 32 embedding dimensions with four heads gives head size eight; the scaled model uses 384 dimensions and six heads of 64 ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:26:59, @1:27:31, @1:39:46). nanoGPT batches all heads inside a single causal self-attention module with a fourth tensor dimension, which is mathematically equivalent but more efficient ([[youtube-2023-01-17-gpt-from-scratch]] @1:47:01, @1:47:36).
