# hn-time-capsule
His project that has an LLM grade decade-old Hacker News discussions with hindsight: GPT 5.1 Thinking analyses every December 2015 front-page thread for what happened and who was most prescient or most wrong ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).

## What it demonstrates
- **Where it came from.** A Hacker News thread in which Gemini 3 hallucinated the front page 10 years ahead had a comment linking the front page from exactly 10 years earlier, December 2015; reading it and mentally grading comments for prescience, he realised "an LLM might actually be a lot better at this task" ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro). He pasted one article and its thread into ChatGPT 5.1 Thinking, found the analysis better and more detailed than his own, and wanted an excuse to vibe code with the newly released Opus 4.5 ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **Why he finds it interesting.** He gives two reasons: he believes it is "quite possible and desirable to train your forward future predictor" given training and effort, without saying whose predictor, and the idea that future LLMs are watching; see [[future-llms-are-watching]] ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **The pipeline.** Given a date, download the front page's 30 articles; for each, fetch the article and the full comment thread through the Algolia API; package everything into a markdown prompt; submit it to GPT 5.1 Thinking via the OpenAI API; collect and parse the results; render static HTML pages, hosted on his site with the intermediate data as a zip file ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **The prompt.** It asks for six sections: a summary, what ended up happening (researched briefly), "Most prescient" and "Most wrong" awards, other notable aspects, grades for specific people, and a final 0–10 interestingness score; sections 5 and 6 have exact formats because he parses them programmatically to accumulate each account's grades over time ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro (prompt)).
- **The results.** A Hall of Fame sorts December 2015 commenters by an IMDb-style score of their grade point average; he congratulates the top ten and points to the noise at the bottom ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro). His example threads include Swift going open source, Figma's launch, the original OpenAI announcement and Theranos struggles ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **Cost.** Thirty-one days of 30 articles meant 930 LLM queries, which cost about $58 and took around an hour; he expects the LLMs of the future to find this kind of thing easier, faster and cheaper ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **Vibe coded.** He says vibe coding it was relatively painless and took about 3 hours with Opus 4.5, with a few hiccups but overall very impressive, and calls the repo "My code (wait, Opus' code?)" ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro). The about-3-hour coding time and the about-1-hour run are separate figures ([[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro).
- **The README (2025-12-10).** The repo pulls the HN front page from exactly 10 years ago, uses an LLM "to evaluate prescience with the benefit of hindsight", and generates an HTML report ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §intro). §What it does lists five steps: fetch the front page from 10 years ago, fetch each article's content and all its comments, generate prompts asking an LLM what happened with hindsight, parse the responses for each commenter's grade, and render an HTML summary ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §What it does). `pipeline.py` has five stages, fetch, prompt, analyze (which "costs money!"), parse and render, runnable together or one by one, with `--limit` for testing, `--date` for a given day and `--model gpt-5-mini` for a cheaper test model; §Files lists a sixth, clean ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §Usage, §Files). Setup is `uv sync` and an OpenAI API key in `.env` ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §Setup).
- **What it stores.** Per day, the front page, the aggregated grades and the HTML report; per article, its metadata, the article text or the fetch error, the comment tree, the full prompt, the LLM's response and the parsed grades ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §Data Directory Structure).
- **The goal.** To find which commenters were most prescient or most wrong over time and surface interesting decade-old predictions; the LLM grades each commenter from A+ to F on how their comments aged, and the Hall of Fame tracks which accounts have the best prediction records ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §What it does, §Example Output). The more general and interesting idea, he says, is "LLMs automatically scouring human artifacts from the past" and synthesising them into knowledge and insights ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §What it does).
- **Unsupported.** "99% of this repo was vibe coded in a few hours with Opus 4.5."; the code is provided as is and he does not intend to support it ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §Vibe code alert). The README says "a few hours" and the blog "about 3 hours", each in its own words ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §Vibe code alert; [[blog-2025-12-10-auto-grade-hn]] §intro). The licence is MIT ([[github-2025-12-10-hn-time-capsule-readme]] §License).
