# llm.c
His codebase for training GPT-2, whose mainline is the CUDA file `train_gpt2.cu`; in its GitHub Discussions he posts occasional State of the Union reports on where the code stands, focusing on that mainline CUDA code ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro). By 2024-05-03 its stated goal is a reliable, stable, clean, tested, minimal, hardened and sufficiently optimised LLM stack that reproduces the GPT-2 miniseries of all sizes, 124M to 1.6B, directly in C/CUDA ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Goal)). He opened the Discussions on 2024-04-12 as a place for people to ask, share and engage without having to create Issues ([[github-2024-04-12-llmc-discussions-welcome]] §llm.c).

## What it demonstrates
- The Discussions are where he posts the project's progress reports; the opening post itself has no technical content ([[github-2024-04-12-llmc-discussions-welcome]] §llm.c).
- **State of the Union, 2024-04-22.** His numbers come from running `profile_gpt2cu.py`; on an A10, 76% of a training step is in NVIDIA cutlass kernels ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro). He measures llm.c at about 6.4% faster per iteration than a PyTorch run in a constrained fp32 setting and 32% lower in peak memory ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro, §intro (peak memory)). `train_gpt2.cu` is at 2097 lines of clean code, with a 2.4 s nvcc compile and 2.2 s from launch to first step ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (lines of code), §intro (latency)).
- The "big stones" he lists as ongoing work: attention through kernel improvement or fusion (for example computing only lower-triangular matmuls), fp16/bf16 in the cleanest way, multi-GPU training with allreduce over MPI or NCCL, and an experimentation harness of argparse, logging and sweeps for bigger runs ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro ("big stones" ongoing work)).
- He credits contributors by handle for kernels and tooling, and the first notable forks are ports by other people to Mojo, C#, Rust and C/Metal ([[github-2024-04-22-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro).

- **State of the Union, 2024-05-03.** He calls it day 24 of the project: llm.c now trains on multiple GPUs, in bfloat16, with flash attention ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro). For the GPT-2 miniseries goal it still needs gradient accumulation, gradient clipping, random initialisation directly in C, learning-rate warmup and schedule, an evaluation (he floats WikiText 103) and a modern pretraining dataset (he floats FineWeb); at the current pace he guessed this was about two weeks out, a forecast rather than a result ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Functional), §intro (Goal)).
- His count of the costs: `train_gpt2.cu` is now about 3,000 lines, plus `common.h` (300) and `tokenizer.h` (100), up from about 2,000 on April 22; nvcc compile time rose to 4.3 s, and about 1m24s with cuDNN flash attention ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Lines of code), §intro (Latency)).
- The fp32 CUDA code was split into `train_gpt2fp32.cu`, to become pure CUDA kernels with no cuBLAS or cuDNN, which he and the contributors think would make a nice endpoint of a CUDA course that starts from the pure-CPU `gpt2.c` ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (fp32 CUDA version plans)).
- He singles out @ngc92 and @ademeure as major contributors, thanks Lambda labs for sponsoring GPUs, lists new ports by others (C++ with CCCL, Zig, Go), and points to #llmc on the nn zero to hero Discord and #llmdotc on the CUDA MODE Discord ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Contributors), §intro (Notable forks), §intro (Acknowledgements)).

- **Toward "production" runs, 2024-05-24.** He says llm.c is getting to the point of serious "production" pretraining runs: training from random initialisation, on a large diverse dataset (FineWeb), evaluated on a standard dataset (HellaSwag), in bfloat16, multi-GPU, with gradient clipping, gradient accumulation and learning-rate scheduling ([[github-2024-05-24-llmc-pytorch-cross-checks]] §intro). In the same post one GPU matches PyTorch's run while the 4-GPU PyTorch run does not yet ([[github-2024-05-24-llmc-pytorch-cross-checks]] §1 GPU training in FP32, §multiGPU).

- **GPT-2 (124M) reproduced, 2024-05-28.** He describes llm.c as about 4,000 lines of C/CUDA and reports training the 12-layer, 12-head, 768-dimension GPT-2 (124M) from scratch on 10B FineWeb tokens on one 8×A100 80GB SXM node in about 90 minutes, about $20 at about $14/hour on Lambda; one GPU would take proportionally longer, which he estimates at roughly 4–24 hours depending on the GPU ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro). He expects significant improvements on that number, since optimisations are pending and nobody has yet tuned the training in the style of "cramming" ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro). The run is exactly 18,865 steps, because one data shard is held out for validation and the batch is exactly 524,288 tokens ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Training)).
- **Code layout.** 95% of the work is in `train_gpt2.cu`, which he says started as a nice clean 1,000 lines of C and is now closer to 3,500, plus four support files; roughly 500 lines set up MPI, NCCL, cuDNN and cuBLAS, 1,500 implement every layer's forward and backward in CUDA (developed in `dev/cuda`), 1,000 are the `gpt2` model's forward and backward, and 1,000 are `main` with the training loop, argument parsing and resume logic ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Code)).
- **350M and next.** Overnight he reproduced the 350M model: 10B tokens was not enough, so he trained about 30B tokens, 60K steps at 820 ms per step, 14 hours, about $200, adding that the plot suggests slightly less might have done ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (350M model)). Next he names the next model size up and then the 1558M "GPT-2", which by his rough napkin math would take about a week and about $2.5K on his single 8×A100 node, an estimate; first he wants the code cleaner, better tested and multi-node ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Coming up)).
- **FAQ.** Sampling works but is inefficient and a bit weird (conditional generation took hacking the prompt tokens into `gen_tokens`); there is no chat, because it is pretraining only; multi-node works in principle through someone else's slurm pull request he has not tried; it is not yet bitwise deterministic, with one kernel left to patch; no fp8 yet; NVIDIA/CUDA only; and CPU users can finetune OpenAI's GPT-2 weights with `train_gpt2.c` ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Sampling), §intro (FAQ)). Decoding samples needs `gpt2_tokenizer.bin`, written by running `train_gpt2.py` on TinyShakespeare ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Tokenizer)).
- **Note:** the 2024-05-03 report forecast the reproduction at "~2 weeks out"; the 124M result is in the Discussion published 2024-05-28 ([[github-2024-05-03-llmc-state-of-the-union]] §intro (Goal); [[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro).
- **Note:** his line that he wants to build the whole thing again from scratch, piece by piece, links to his 2024-02-21 post on building the ramp ([[github-2024-05-28-llmc-gpt-2-124m]] §intro (Coming up); [[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]).

- **In the GPT-2 lecture, 2024-06-09.** He presents llm.c as a pure CUDA implementation of GPT-2/GPT-3 training with a nanoGPT-like PyTorch reference file ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:56:27, @3:57:01). It gives the same losses and norms as the PyTorch version but runs faster, starts faster and takes less space; he notes it is specific to GPT-2/3 while PyTorch is general ([[youtube-2024-06-09-reproduce-gpt-2]] @3:57:37, @3:58:11, @3:58:46, @3:59:19).

- **GPT-2 (774M), 2024-06-12.** He ran the 774M model for about six days on his 8×A100 80GB node, 150B tokens or 1.5 epochs of the 100B FineWeb sample, and it finished with no major issues; the launch script is `scripts/run_gpt2_774M.sh`, the final log and checkpoint are on S3, and a Hugging Face upload is credited to another user, `mdouglas` ([[github-2024-06-12-llmc-gpt-2-774m]] §intro, §intro (s3 paths), §intro (huggingface paths)). Next he wants to figure out the evals, then launch "the GPT-2", 1558M, on a bigger node so it does not take half a month, and use it to merge multi-GPU pull requests ([[github-2024-06-12-llmc-gpt-2-774m]] §intro (Next up)).

- **GPT-2 (1558M), 2024-07-11.** llm.c reproduces the full 1558M GPT-2 of OpenAI's 2019 release directly in C/CUDA, about 5,000 lines, without Python or a library such as PyTorch, JAX or huggingface/transformers, on one 8×H100 node in 24 hours for $672 ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro). His caveats: not perfectly tuned, not sufficiently stabilised, evals not comprehensive ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro). From a blank box it takes about a minute to start stepping; one GPU gives the same model in about 8 days, 16 GPUs in about 12 hours ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Training)). On OpenAI's unicorn prompt the completion is fairly coherent and, he judges, qualitatively around GPT-2's level ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Unicorns)).
- **Release and FAQ.** The final model is on S3 and Hugging Face, with longer 100K- and 330K-step versions also uploaded; export to Hugging Face format enables the Eleuther harness and sampling ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (The final model), §intro (Model export)). Sampling in llm.c is inefficient and hacky, there is no chat (pretraining only), fp8 is in progress, and it is CUDA only, with an actively maintained AMD fork by @anthonix ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (FAQ)). He names @ngc92, @ademeure, @gordicaleksa and @rosslwheeler as the core contributors, Lambda as the GPU sponsor, and Nvidia and Ubicloud for CI runners ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Authors)).
- **Roadmap and goal.** Next: tune GPT-2 hyperparameters, improve training and scaling stability, fp8, inference with a KV cache, SFT and RLHF, multimodal extensions, and Llama/Gemma support; the goal remains a simple, minimal, clean training stack for a full-featured LLM agent in direct C/CUDA, with companion educational materials ([[github-2024-07-11-llmc-gpt-2-1-6b]] §intro (Coming up)).
