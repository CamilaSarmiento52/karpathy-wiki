# To get the most out of current tools, he says, you have to take yourself out of the loop and arrange things to run autonomously
> "I shouldn't be a bottleneck." — [[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @18:36

## Context
Asked about the motivation for autoresearch, he recalls tweeting that to get the most out of the tools now available you have to remove yourself as the bottleneck: you cannot be there to prompt the next thing, and must arrange things so they are completely autonomous ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @16:28). The name of the game, he says, is to increase your leverage: he puts in very few tokens once in a while and a huge amount happens on his behalf ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @16:28, @17:00). He frames the question as how to get more agents running for longer periods without his involvement, and calls [[autoresearch]] an example of that implication: he does not want to be the researcher in the loop looking at results, holding the system back ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @17:00, @17:32). He feels nervous when he has subscription quota left over, because it means he has not maximised his token throughput, as he felt as a PhD student when his GPUs were idle ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @05:30). For the frontier labs he says researchers can contribute ideas but should not be enacting them: a single queue of ideas, workers that try them, and a feature branch that people merge from sometimes ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @19:40, @20:13).

He attaches caveats: this suits tasks with objective metrics that are easy to evaluate, see [[autoresearch-needs-an-objective-metric]], and the whole thing still has cracks, so going too far ahead is net not useful ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @23:29, @24:02).
