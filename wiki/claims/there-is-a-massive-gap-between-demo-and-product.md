# In self-driving, and he expects in AGI, there is a massive gap between a demo and a product, and then between a product and its globalisation
> "demo and product there's a massive Gap there" — [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39

## Context
His first Waymo ride was a near-perfect demo, "2014 or so" by his recollection, and it took ten years to become a city-scale product he can pay for ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:07). Asked whether the delay was regulatory or technical, he says a 30-minute demo drive does not show everything the team had to deal with for a decade, and that a lot of it was also regulatory ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39). He adds a second gap: globalisation has not happened at all, the world has not changed yet, and going from a demo to its globalisation will take a long time; he suspects AGI will look similar ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39, @02:11). He says AGI here, not agents ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @02:11).

Later in the interview he uses the same contrast for AI in education: many capabilities are a prompt away, so you always get demos, but "the demo is near but the product is far"; adapting to a learner's background needs work to actually work rather than demo ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @35:35, @36:08).
