# In self-driving, and he expects in AGI, there is a massive gap between a demo and a product, and then between a product and its globalisation
> "demo and product there's a massive Gap there" — [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39

## Context
His first Waymo ride was a near-perfect demo, "2014 or so" by his recollection, and it took ten years to become a city-scale product he can pay for ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:07). Asked whether the delay was regulatory or technical, he says a 30-minute demo drive does not show everything the team had to deal with for a decade, and that a lot of it was also regulatory ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39). He adds a second gap: globalisation has not happened at all, the world has not changed yet, and going from a demo to its globalisation will take a long time; he suspects AGI will look similar ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:39, @02:11). He says AGI here, not agents ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @02:11).

Later in the interview he uses the same contrast for AI in education: many capabilities are a prompt away, so you always get demos, but "the demo is near but the product is far"; adapting to a learner's background needs work to actually work rather than demo ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @35:35, @36:08).

**The march of nines (Dwarkesh, published 2025-10-17).** He says he gets many intuitions from five years on self-driving, whose first demos go back to a CMU truck in 1986 ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:43:43, @1:44:22). His Waymo demo is now "in 2014 or something like that", a perfect drive around Palo Alto, after which it still took a long time; tasks with a high cost of failure have a very large demo-to-product gap ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:44:22, @1:45:02). Software engineering shares that property, he says, though a lot of vibe coding does not ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:45:02, @1:45:36). What takes the time is a march of nines: a demo that works 90% of the time is the first nine, and "Every single nine is the same amount of work." ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:45:36). At Tesla, over about five years, they went through maybe two or three nines, with more to go; he is extremely unimpressed by demos, and this reinforced his timelines ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:46:16, @1:46:50). **Note:** the year of the Waymo demo is "2014 or so" in No Priors, 2013 in the YC talk and "2014 or something like that" here; each is his own recollection, not reconciled ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:44:22; [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @01:07; [[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @26:01).
