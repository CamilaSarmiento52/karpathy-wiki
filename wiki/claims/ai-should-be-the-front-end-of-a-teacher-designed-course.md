# With current models, the teacher should design the course and AI should be the front end to the student
> "I don't think the models are good enough to create a good course" — [[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:21

## Context
He is building a single course, the one you would go to if you want to learn AI, and asks how to scale a course like CS231n, which he taught at Stanford and calls the first deep learning class, to perhaps 8 billion people in many languages and at all levels ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @31:50, @32:21). Current models are not good enough to create a good course, he says, but they are good enough to be the front end to the student and interpret the course for them ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:21). The teacher moves to the back end and designs the materials, while the AI is the front end, speaks every language and takes you through the course; an AI teaching assistant is one way to think of it ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:54). He says this is tractable today, does not exist yet, can be made really good, and can be refactored as capability grows ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @32:54, @33:26). He likes finding things that match today's AI capability, and says many companies misjudge it, building either too far ahead or not ambitiously enough ([[talk-2024-09-05-no-priors-ep-80]] @33:26).
