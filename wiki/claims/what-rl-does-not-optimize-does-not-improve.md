# What RL does not optimise, such as telling jokes, he says has not improved, so models stay jagged
> "It's outside of what's being improved." — [[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @26:44

## Context
He says he simultaneously feels he is talking to an extremely brilliant PhD student who has been a systems programmer all their life and a 10-year-old, and that agents are much more jagged than humans ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @24:34). Sometimes he asks for functionality and gets something totally wrong, and he is still frustrated with agents all the time; the lines that follow in @25:05 are probably the host's ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @25:05). His hypothesis is that the models are trained by reinforcement learning, so labs can improve anything verifiable or with rewards, such as whether the unit tests pass, while nuance, what he intended and when to ask clarifying questions are worse ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @25:39). You are either on rails, part of the "super intelligence circuits", or outside the verifiable domains, where everything meanders ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @26:11). Ask a state-of-the-art model for a joke and you get the same atoms joke as three or four years ago, while on an agentic task it will go for hours, because jokes are outside the RL ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @26:11, @26:44). Asked about the premise that being smarter at code should make models better at everything, he says he does not think that is happening, perhaps a little but not a satisfying amount ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @27:51).

## Related claims
- Supports [[verifiability-drives-the-jagged-frontier]]: on 2025-11-17 he wrote that verifiable tasks progress rapidly while what is not verifiable is left to generalisation or weaker means, which drives the jagged frontier ([[blog-2025-11-17-verifiability]] §intro); here the same split explains why jokes have not improved while agentic tasks have ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @25:39, @26:44).
