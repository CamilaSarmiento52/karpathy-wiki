# Llama and Mistral are open-weights models, not open source
> "I wouldn't actually say they're open source right" — [[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:27

## Context
Asked how the ecosystem of models will play out, he says we have to be careful with naming: models such as Llama and Mistral are, to him, like being tossed a binary of an operating system, useful but not fully ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:27, @07:59). Fully open-source LLMs release the entire infrastructure needed to compile the operating system, that is, to gather the data and train the model ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @07:59). The difference matters in practice: with only the weights, the more you finetune the more the model regresses on everything else, and avoiding that by training on a mixture of old and new data needs the dataset and training loop ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @08:32). He proposes three categories, open-weights, open-source and proprietary models ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @09:05). Later he says Meta has so far only released open weights and should go further ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @31:06).
