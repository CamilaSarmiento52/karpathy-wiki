# In 2023 he did not love autoregressive sampling and found diffusion more appealing
> "I certainly don't love the auto aggressive modeling approach" — [[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @50:15

## Context
Asked in the CS25 Q&A about autoregressive modelling, he says it seems weird to him to sample a token and then commit to it ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @50:15). He suggests hybrids with diffusion, which he would find really cool, or other ways to edit sequences later while staying in the autoregressive framework ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @50:15). He personally finds diffusion, which he calls up-and-coming, much more appealing, because when he writes he does a first draft and then a better second draft, which feels like a diffusion process, rather than committing chunk by chunk ([[talk-2023-05-19-stanford-cs25-transformers]] @50:15, @50:48).

On 2024-03-26, asked about open research problems, he names the split between diffusion and autoregressive models, both ways of representing probability distributions, each apparently a good fit for different modalities; he thinks there is probably space to unify or connect them in a hybrid, and says it feels wrong to him that there is nothing in between ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @12:22, @12:55). He returns to the autoregressive-or-diffusion setup as a place with some fruit probably ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @34:24).
