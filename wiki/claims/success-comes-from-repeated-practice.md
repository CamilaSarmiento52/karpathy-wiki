# To a very large extent, success comes from repeated practice, on the order of 10,000 hours
> "success comes from just repeated practice" — [[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @16:04

## Context
He says he is quite a big believer in the 10,000 hours popularised by Malcolm Gladwell, the idea being Gladwell's and the belief his: to a very large extent success comes from a huge amount of repeated practice ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @16:04). His advice is to count the hours rather than worry about whether you are succeeding or failing; projects that failed and did not snowball still added to his counter ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @16:04, @16:41).

His first example is awesomemovies.life, a weekend movie-recommendation site that a reply on X found impressive; to him it was about his 20th site like it, Linode, a Flask server, scraping, TF-IDF vectors and an SVM, remixed from code snippets of earlier projects ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @16:41, @17:16, @17:53). His second: when he joined Tesla Autopilot to lead computer vision, about seven or eight years before the talk, he rewrote the vision network's training codebase from scratch in PyTorch in his first months; it became the kernel of what exists now, and it did not feel impressive to him after five PhD years of doing the same ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @17:53, @18:33, @19:11). Things that look impressive, he says, look much less so once you have done them 20 times ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @19:11). At six hours a day, he works out 10,000 hours as about five years, roughly the length of a PhD ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @19:11, @19:48).
