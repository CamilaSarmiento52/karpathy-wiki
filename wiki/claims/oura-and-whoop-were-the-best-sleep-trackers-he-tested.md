# In his two-month test, Oura and Whoop were the best sleep trackers and AutoSleep was near random
> "AutoSleep is basically a random number generator." — [[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Shopping recommendations)

## Context
**Why he measured.** After a Bryan Johnson video on sleep habits, which he says he came across about two months before the post, he resolved to improve his sleep and to measure it first; the habits in the video are Johnson's ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro).

**How he compared them.** His procedure, in the order the post gives it ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro):
1. He wore Oura, Whoop, an 8Sleep Pod 4 Ultra and an Apple Watch with AutoSleep at the same time for about two months ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro).
2. Every morning right after waking he logged each device's sleep stages, resting heart rate, HRV and sleep score in a simple spreadsheet, which he links, to compare them and relate them to how he felt that morning ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro).
3. He plotted a histogram of each tracker's scores to see saturation and dynamic range ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Qualitative assessment)).
4. He correlated the trackers' scores pairwise to see which agree most ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Qualitative assessment)).
5. He checked resting heart rate across trackers and against sleep score, warning that this is just correlation analysis and that he does not know whether the apps use resting heart rate in their score ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Matters of Heart Rate)).

**What he found.** He rates Oura and Whoop "Tier 1", fairly similar and high quality, with scores that mostly matched how he felt in the morning; 8Sleep ok; and Apple Watch with AutoSleep really terrible, its scores almost random and swinging wildly ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Qualitative assessment)). AutoSleep and 8Sleep give very high scores that push against 100, Whoop is a little too easy to please, and Oura is the hardest to please, with the most dynamic range, which he counts in its favour; his best Oura score was 92, while he gets 100 on Whoop fairly regularly ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Qualitative assessment)). The pairwise score correlations run from Whoop–Oura 0.65 down to Whoop–AutoSleep 0.14, and if Whoop is good, which he thinks it is, AutoSleep looks almost like a noise generator ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Qualitative assessment)).

**Heart rate.** He says all trackers except 8Sleep agree quite highly on night-time heart rate, 8Sleep being a mattress without a direct measurement ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Matters of Heart Rate)). **Note:** the correlation matrix he prints gives 8Sleep 0.947 with both AutoSleep and Oura and puts the lowest pair at Whoop–8Sleep, 0.879; the wiki reports both and does not reconcile them ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Matters of Heart Rate)). Over the 60 days, with about 30 minutes of Zone 2 a day on average and a 4x4x4 HIIT session twice a week, his resting heart rate fell a bit less than 3 bpm, from about 51 to about 48, and his HRV rose from about 49 to 54 ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Matters of Heart Rate)).

**His recommendation.** Oura has an excellent app with one dense overview, but the ring is thick, gets in the way of his grip at the gym and must come off to charge; Whoop has an excellent, more click-heavy app, saturates at 100 fairly easily, and has a better wrist form factor with a charger that attaches so it never comes off ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Shopping recommendations)). His advice: Whoop for 9.5/10 reliable, convenient tracking, Oura for 10/10 tracking if you are fine with the ring, and not AutoSleep, though a better Apple Watch app may exist that he has not found ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Shopping recommendations)).

**Sleep and work.** He says with absolute certainty that his sleep scores correlate strongly with the quality of work he can do that day: with a low score he lacks agency, courage and creativity, and with a high one he can power through anything ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Yes, sleep matters)). What matters is accumulated sleep debt over the last few days, so one bad night is usually fine and several in a row are bad ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Yes, sleep matters)). He has not yet seen a dramatic improvement in his own sleep over the two months and treats it as a year-long project ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Matters of Heart Rate)). The verdicts are his as of 2025-03-24 ([[blog-2025-03-24-best-sleep-tracker]] §intro (Shopping recommendations)).
