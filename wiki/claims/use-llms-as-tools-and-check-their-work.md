# Use LLMs as tools: for inspiration and first drafts, check their work and own the result
> "use it as a tool in a toolbox don't trust it fully" — [[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:30:23

## Context
After the 9.11-versus-9.9 failure he calls an LLM a stochastic system that is really magical but that you cannot fully trust, to be used as a tool rather than let rip on a problem with the results copy-pasted ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @2:07:12). Summing up, he says not to treat the models as infallible: check their work, use them for inspiration and first drafts, and be ultimately responsible for the product of your work ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:08:07, @3:09:14). He uses them tens or hundreds of times a day because they dramatically accelerate his work, but says even RL models will randomly do dumb things, hallucinate or skip mental arithmetic, so check and verify ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:30:23, @3:30:56). Looking ahead, he expects people to supervise agents carrying out long tasks, with human-to-agent ratios ([[youtube-2025-02-05-deep-dive-into-llms]] @3:11:26, @3:12:00, @3:12:33).

**How I use LLMs, 2025-02-27.** He applies the same stance to newer tools: treat a deep research report as a first draft and as papers to look at, since any of it can be hallucinated or misunderstood ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @48:26). He always reads the code Advanced Data Analysis writes and would hesitate to recommend such tools to people who cannot read and verify it ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:05:46, @1:06:19). His checks are the method [[verify-citations]] ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @48:26).
