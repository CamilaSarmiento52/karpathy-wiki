# He says US chemical and food regulation lags far behind the industry and behind Europe
> "The government is significantly lagging behind the industry on chemical regulation" — [[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (TLDR)

## Context
He says the food industry has a clear financial incentive to trade something 10% cheaper for something 10X more harmful, as long as the harm shows up over a period long enough that the accounting is impractical; the numbers are his illustration, not measurements ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food)). "Sadly, the US Government has been woefully inadequate in constraining the industry", he writes, lagging far behind other countries, Europe especially, and he links the recent MAHA efforts as following from this, with no further view on them ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Food)). He says there is not enough testing, scrutiny and oversight of these deep supply chains ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (Learn more)). In his TLDR the government lags the industry on chemical regulation, so the responsibility is yours ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (TLDR)).

He says the EU bans or restricts many food additives, dyes, chemicals and processing practices that are routine in the US, and that the FDA's GRAS system lets manufacturers self-certify ingredients without independent review, a new chemical being innocent until proven guilty, while in Europe the default is often the reverse ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (closing)). His frustrated tone, he says, comes from the government forcing him to be a part-time investigative journalist so as not to feel he is poisoning himself and his family; he adds that he is not a professional toxicologist or food scientist ([[blog-2025-12-18-chemical-hygiene]] §intro (closing)).
