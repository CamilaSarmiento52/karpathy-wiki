# He agrees with Sutton that frontier LLMs are not platonically bitter-lesson pilled, though perhaps practically so
> "They are not platonically bitter lesson pilled" — [[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro

## Context
Responding to Richard Sutton on the Dwarkesh podcast, he says Sutton's criticism of LLMs as not bitter-lesson pilled "is not inadequate" ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro). **Note:** the double negative reads as agreement rather than strong endorsement. His reasons are that frontier LLMs carry human input at every stage: pretraining data is all human text, finetuning data is human and curated, and the RL environment mixture is tuned by human engineers ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro). We do not have a single clean, bitter-lesson-pilled "turn the crank" algorithm that learns from experience alone, and finding one would be a huge breakthrough ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro).

He still thinks the bitter lesson is correct, but more as something platonic to pursue than necessarily to reach in practice, and says so with double-digit percent uncertainty ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro). Compared with much of what came before, LLMs are perhaps "practically" bitter-lesson pilled ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro). His conclusion is hedged: "Probably we are still not sufficiently bitter lesson pilled" ([[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro).

**Note:** the `§intro` text cited here was also posted on X the same day, word for word; that post is a second location of the same words, not independent evidence, and has no Appendix ([[x-2025-10-01-sutton-podcast]] #1; [[blog-2025-10-01-animals-vs-ghosts]] §intro).
