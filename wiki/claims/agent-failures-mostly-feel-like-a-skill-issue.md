# When agents fail, it mostly feels to him like a skill issue rather than missing capability, though he says it is hard to tell
> "It's not that the capability is not there." — [[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @03:20

## Context
Asked what limits what he can explore, he says that when things do not work it largely feels like a skill issue: he has not found how to string together what is available, gave poor enough instructions in the agents file, or lacks a good enough memory tool ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @03:20, @03:53). He says it "all kind of feels like skill issue when it doesn't work to some extent" ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @03:53). Being a skill issue is empowering, he says, because you could be getting better, which is why he finds it addictive; the sentences just before this at @06:02 may be the host's and are not attributed to him ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @06:02). The layers keep stacking, LLMs, agents, claw-like entities, multiples of them, instructions to them and optimisation over the instructions, which is why he calls it infinite and everything a skill issue (the caption says "scale issue") ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @22:57).

He hedges it himself: you cannot let it go all the way yet because it does not fully work, or it is a skill issue and we have not figured out how to use it; "it's hard to tell" ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @28:24). He still names capability limits in the same interview: the whole thing is bursting at the seams and going too far ahead is net not useful ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @24:02).
