# He imagines, as a hedged extrapolation, neural nets becoming the host process with CPUs as coprocessors
> "the neural net becomes kind of like the host process" — [[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @08:40

## Context
Asked what will look obvious in hindsight, he extrapolates from MenuGen: a lot of code shouldn't exist and neural networks do most of the work, and the extrapolation "looks very weird": one could imagine completely neural computers, a device taking raw video or audio into a neural net that uses diffusion to render a UI unique to that moment ([[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @08:07). In the 1950s and 60s, he says, it was not obvious whether computers would look like calculators or neural nets; we went the calculator way, and neural nets now run virtualised on classical computers ([[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @08:40). He imagines a lot of this flipping, the neural net as host process and CPUs as coprocessors, with intelligence compute becoming the dominant spend of flops, referring to a diagram shown earlier at the event ([[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @08:40). Neural nets would do the heavy lifting and use tools as a historical appendage for deterministic tasks; we will probably get there piece by piece, and the progression "is TBD" ([[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @09:13). It is an extrapolation he frames as weird and foreign, not a forecast with a date ([[talk-2026-04-29-sequoia-ascent-2026]] @08:07, @09:13).
