# This is the decade of agents, not the year
> "this is the decade of agents" — [[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:09

## Context
He says claims that 2025 is "the year of agents" concern him ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:09). His evidence is self-driving: a perfect Waymo demo drive in 2013, and twelve years later the problem still needs teleoperation and humans in the loop ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @26:01, @26:35, @27:09). He concludes that software is tricky in the same way, so agents need humans in the loop and careful work ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:44).

## Related claims
- Supported by [[claude-code-is-the-first-convincing-llm-agent]]: in his 2025-12-19 review he describes an intermediate, slow-enough takeoff in which agents make most sense on the developer's own computer ([[blog-2025-12-19-year-in-review-2025]] §4). **Note:** the talk itself does not discuss where agents run ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:09).
- Supported, on its human-in-the-loop half only, by [[use-llms-as-copilots-with-human-oversight]]: in State of GPT (May 2023) he already advised copilots under human oversight rather than autonomous agents, because it was not clear the models were there ([[talk-2023-05-25-state-of-gpt]] @39:57); here he says agents need humans in the loop and will take a decade ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:09, @27:44). Consistent caution, not a change of view; the 2023 talk gives no timescale.
- Supported, weakly, by [[llm-products-will-take-years-to-figure-out]]: in the Sequoia interview (published 2024-03-26) he says LLM systems are quite autonomous but not fully, with oversight and evaluation to be worked out over the next few years ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @06:54); here agents need humans in the loop over a decade ([[youtube-2025-06-19-software-is-changing]] @27:09, @27:44). Same direction; the 2024 horizon is a few years, not a decade.
