# Running these models is probably a thousand to a million times less efficient than a brain
> "probably off by a factor of a thousand to like a million" — [[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @13:27

## Context
Asked which research problems keep him up at night, he names the gap in energetic efficiency: his brain runs on roughly 20 watts, while the supercomputers being built are measured in megawatts ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @12:55). He hedges that he does not know how much you need exactly to run a brain, but thinks it safe to say we are off by a factor of a thousand to a million, and repeats the range later ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @12:55, @13:27, @14:33). He puts part of the gap down to computers not designed for this workload, and names architecture adapted to it, lower precision, sparsity and moving beyond the von Neumann architecture as levers ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @13:27, @13:59, @14:33).
