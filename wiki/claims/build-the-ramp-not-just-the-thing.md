# After building the thing, build the ramp: making work accessible is necessary, not optional
> "Step 2 build the ramp." — [[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]

## Context
His example is micrograd: the repo got some traction and then stagnated for many months, and only after he made the video building it from scratch did it go through hockey-stick growth, with the code itself unchanged ([[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]). He had thought the short, commented code and README were self-explanatory; he concludes what stood in the way was accessibility, since the video almost 100X'd interest from beginners and from expert friends he thinks were deterred by a barrier to entry ([[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]). He generalises that technical people have a strong bias to put up code, papers or the final thing, assume it is mostly self-explanatory, and blame low engagement on the thing not being good enough ([[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]). In reality there is still a large barrier to engaging with your work, even for other experts, and you may be leaving somewhere around 10–100X of its potential on the table; a voice in your head will say the ramp is not necessary, and he says it is wrong ([[x-2024-02-21-technical-accessibility]]).

In the Sequoia interview, asked what would make the AI ecosystem more vibrant, he cites the post: there are a lot of people building the thing and a lot less happening in building ramps so that people can understand it ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @32:13). Because everyone is new to this and needs to ramp up, he would love people to be more open about what they learned, how they trained and what worked ([[talk-2024-03-26-sequoia-making-ai-accessible]] @32:45).
