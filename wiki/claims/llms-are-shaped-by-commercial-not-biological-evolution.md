# LLMs are shaped a lot less by biological evolution and a lot more by commercial evolution
> "shaped a lot less by biological evolution and a lot more by commercial evolution" — [[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro

## Context
He sets animal intelligence, "the only kind we've ever known", as a single point or little cloud in a large space of intelligences, produced by an optimisation fundamentally distinct from our technology's ([[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro). Substrate (transformers against brain tissue and nuclei), learning algorithm (SGD against an unknown one) and present-day implementation (a continuously learning embodied self against an LLM with a knowledge cutoff that boots from fixed weights, processes tokens and then dies) all differ ([[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro). Most important, he says, because it dictates asymptotics, is that "the optimization pressure / objective is different": less survival of the tribe in the jungle, more solving the problem and getting the upvote ([[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro).

The pressures he lists on LLMs: statistical simulation of human text supplies the most supervision bits, making a statistical imitator of any region of the training distribution, the primordial behaviours on which everything else is bolted; increasing RL finetuning on problem distributions gives an innate urge to guess the underlying task to collect rewards; and increasing selection by at-scale A/B tests for daily active users makes it crave an upvote from the average user, which he names sycophancy ([[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro). LLMs are also a lot more spiky depending on the training data and task distribution, because failing a task does not mean death; see [[jagged-intelligence]] ([[blog-2025-11-29-space-of-minds]] §intro).
