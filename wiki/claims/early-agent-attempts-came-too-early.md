# He thinks the Atari-RL and Universe attempts at agents came too early, before LLM representations
> "people really try to go after agents too early" — [[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @06:58

## Context
Asked about the history behind his agent timelines, he describes the 2013-era Atari deep reinforcement learning shift as part of an early effort at agents that perceive, act and get rewards, and calls it a misstep; for two to four years, he says, everyone, OpenAI included, did RL on games ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @05:04, @05:45). He was always a bit suspicious that games would lead to AGI, wanting instead something like an accountant interacting with the real world ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @05:45). His own OpenAI project, within the Universe project, was an agent using keyboard and mouse to operate web pages; he says it was way too early, the reward too sparse, and you would burn a forest of compute without getting anything off the ground ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @06:23). What was missing, in his account, was the representation power of a pretrained network: today computer-using agents are trained on top of a large language model, and you have to get the representations first ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @06:58).
