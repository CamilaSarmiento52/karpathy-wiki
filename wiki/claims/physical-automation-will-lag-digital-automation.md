# Automation of physical infrastructure will probably lag digital automation, because bits are easier than atoms
> "slightly lagging behind a lot of the digital Innovations" — [[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42

## Context
Right after the LLM OS analogy, he says that while the film "Her" focuses on emotional intelligence, these models in practice will probably be doing a ton of problem solving in the digital space ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:04). He pictures not one digital entity but many, given tasks, talking to and collaborating with each other and automating a lot of digital infrastructure ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:04). Physical infrastructure may follow, but he says it is at an earlier stage and will probably lag the digital innovations, because it is much easier to work with bits than atoms ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42). Pointing to "I, Robot", set in 2035, he says that in ten years you can squint and imagine robots performing tasks in the physical world, and asks how we would program them and make sure they do what we want ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42, @09:19). The claim is hedged ("probably") and gives no timescale for the digital side ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42).

In the Dwarkesh interview, published 2025-10-17, he expects more and more autonomous entities doing digital work and physical work "some amount of time later" ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:18:29). Granting Dwarkesh's point that serving another copy of a model costs far less than building another car, he says bits are a million times easier than anything that touches the physical world, so he would expect much faster adaptation in digital industry ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:52:56, @1:53:31). The same view, stated more strongly, not a change of view ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @1:53:31).

In the Skill Issue interview, published 2026-03-20, he restates it with reasons: flipping bits and copy-pasting digital information makes everything a million times faster than accelerating matter, so he expects a huge amount of activity in the digital space and the physical world to be behind by some amount of time ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @39:17, @39:50, @40:22). Self-driving, which he calls the first robotics application, taught him that robotics needs a lot of capital, time and conviction ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @54:37). Atoms, he says, are a million times harder and will lag, the interfaces between digital and physical, sensors and actuators, come next, and the physical market may be bigger ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @55:09, @56:15, @56:48, @57:21). The same view again, not a change ([[talk-2026-03-20-skill-issue]] @57:21).
