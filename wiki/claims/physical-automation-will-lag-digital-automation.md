# Automation of physical infrastructure will probably lag digital automation, because bits are easier than atoms
> "slightly lagging behind a lot of the digital Innovations" — [[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42

## Context
Right after the LLM OS analogy, he says that while the film "Her" focuses on emotional intelligence, these models in practice will probably be doing a ton of problem solving in the digital space ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:04). He pictures not one digital entity but many, given tasks, talking to and collaborating with each other and automating a lot of digital infrastructure ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:04). Physical infrastructure may follow, but he says it is at an earlier stage and will probably lag the digital innovations, because it is much easier to work with bits than atoms ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42). Pointing to "I, Robot", set in 2035, he says that in ten years you can squint and imagine robots performing tasks in the physical world, and asks how we would program them and make sure they do what we want ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42, @09:19). The claim is hedged ("probably") and gives no timescale for the digital side ([[talk-2024-07-03-berkeley-hackathon-keynote]] @08:42).
