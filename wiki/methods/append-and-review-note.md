# Append-and-review note
His note-taking approach of many years, which he calls the "append-and-review note" and finds super simple while covering most of his day-to-day note-taking; he does not claim to have invented it ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro).

## Steps
1. **One note.** He keeps a single text note called "notes" in Apple Notes, which handles offline editing, syncing between devices and backup ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Data structure)). Why one note is [[one-text-note-beats-many-organized-notes]] ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Data structure)).
2. **Append at the top.** Any idea, todo or anything else goes on top as plain text, from his work computer or his phone on the go ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Append)).
3. **No metadata.** He does not find dates, links, concepts or tags useful and does not add them by default; the exception is a "watch:", "listen:" or "read:" prefix he can search for when he wants something to watch, listen to or read ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Append)).
4. **Let it sink, then skim.** New items push old ones down almost as if under gravity; every now and then he scrolls down and skims ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Review)).
5. **Rescue.** Anything that deserves attention he copy-pastes back to the top ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Review)).
6. **Merge, rarely delete.** Sometimes he merges, groups or modifies related notes, and deletes one only rarely; notes that keep failing review keep sinking but are never lost ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Review)). Re-ordering notes, he sometimes finds he has thought about the same thing from different perspectives, and merging them gives him additional insight ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Example usage)).

His examples of what goes in: a random idea on the go, a movie or book recommendation, a morning TODO list, a draft tweet that needs more thought, a quote, numbers from a paper, a shell command he keeps forgetting, the commands and outcome of a hyperparameter sweep, and a list of worries when stressed ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro (Example usage)). Writing something down lets him wipe his working memory and focus fully elsewhere, confident he will revisit it at review; over the years the note has grown giant, and sometimes an old idea becomes relevant in a new light ([[blog-2025-03-19-append-and-review-note]] §intro).
