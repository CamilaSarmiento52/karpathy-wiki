# Choose a model tier
How he picks which LLM, tier and mode to use for a question, as he describes his own practice in How I use LLMs; his takeaway is to be mindful of the models you are using ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @20:07). **Note:** [[make-it-work-then-make-it-cheap]] also starts from the most capable model, but for building an application, not personal use ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @21:13).

## Steps
1. **Check the model.** He looks at the model selector; a logged-out user may get a smaller model, and OpenAI does not say which ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @17:53, @18:26). The free tier's GPT-4o mini is smaller, less creative, knows less and probably hallucinates more ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @19:01).
2. **Compare tiers.** Bigger models cost more to run, so companies charge more for them; decide whether the cheaper tiers are enough ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @20:07). He gives the ChatGPT Pro price as $100 per month at one point and as $200 per month at others ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @19:34, @26:52, @44:02).
3. **Pay up for professional work.** He pays for ChatGPT Pro and Claude's professional plan because for his coding work it is still very cheap, and says to consider top tiers if you use the models professionally ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @20:07, @20:40). Other providers have the same tier structure, for example paying enough to get Grok 3 rather than Grok 2 ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @20:40, @21:48).
4. **Use the most powerful model that works for you.** In his words, use the most powerful model that kind of works for you ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @21:13). Large models have more world knowledge and write better, while small ones hallucinate more ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @2:07:46, @2:08:18).
5. **Non-thinking first.** Thinking models, tuned with RL, help most on difficult math and code; he usually starts with a non-thinking model because it is fast, and switches to a thinking model when he suspects the answer could be better ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @29:41, @30:13, @2:08:18). His example: a gradient-check bug that GPT-4o did not solve and o1 pro did after about a minute of thinking ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @25:44, @27:25).
6. **Ask a council.** He often pays for several providers and asks them all the same question, calling them his "llm Council" ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @21:48, @22:22).
7. **Know each model's tools.** Different LLMs have different tools: without a code interpreter, Grok 3 did a large multiplication in its head and got it slightly wrong, and Gemini 2.0 Pro was close but wrong on a harder one ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @1:02:24, @1:02:58, @1:03:32, @1:04:06). Features also differ between web and mobile apps ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @2:10:31).
8. **Default, then specialise.** He calls ChatGPT the incumbent, the most popular and most feature-rich app, and a very good default, while other apps reach parity or overtake it in specific cases; his per-task picks include Perplexity for search, Claude artifacts for prototypes and diagrams, ChatGPT's advanced voice for talking, and Grok when ChatGPT is too cagey ([[youtube-2025-02-27-how-i-use-llms]] @01:08, @2:06:40, @2:07:13).
