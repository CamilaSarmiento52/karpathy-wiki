# Learn by building and explaining
How he says he learns and teaches technical material: build it from scratch, find the first-order piece and ramp up from it, and explain it to find the gaps; he gives it as his practice and his advice to students, in the Dwarkesh interview ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @29:27, @2:17:33, @2:24:31).

## Steps
1. **Rebuild without copy-paste.** To learn from nanochat he would put it on the right monitor and build it from the start, allowed to reference it but not to copy-paste; building from scratch forces you to face what you do not know you do not understand; see [[if-you-cannot-build-it-you-do-not-understand-it]] ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @28:48, @29:27, @30:04).
2. **Find the first-order term.** Trained by physics in first-, second- and third-order approximations, he looks for the first-order component of a system and the simplest thing that shows it in action, then tacks on the other terms ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:16:18, @2:17:33). His example is micrograd, about 100 lines of interpretable Python that run forward and backward through arbitrary neural networks, not efficiently; everything else, he says, is efficiency ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:18:10, @2:18:43).
3. **Build a ramp.** He lays the tangle of understanding out as a ramp where everything depends only on the thing before it, which he calls the most intellectually interesting part of education ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:18:43). Each piece should come with the problem it solves; taking the student through that progression is his reply to Dwarkesh's phrase about presenting the pain before the solution ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:19:57).
4. **Let them try first.** He prompts students to guess before giving the solution, because trying first shows the action space and the objective, and maximises the knowledge per new fact added ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:19:57, @2:20:34).
5. **On demand and breadth-wise.** As a learner he alternates depth-wise learning on demand, for a project that pays off with a reward, with breadth-wise "101" learning of the kind school does ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:23:51, @2:24:31).
6. **Explain to learn.** Explaining things to people shows him what he does not understand, because he cannot explain it, and forces him to go back and fill the gaps; he loves to re-explain things ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:24:31, @2:25:09).
7. **Say it as over lunch.** Against the curse of knowledge, he would like people to share their naive ChatGPT conversations about his material, and he finds that how someone would explain their paper over lunch is more understandable and more accurate than the paper ([[talk-2025-10-17-dwarkesh-ghosts-not-animals]] @2:21:16, @2:21:54, @2:22:33).
