# Finally had a chance to listen through this pod with Sutton, which was…

## Summary
He reports Richard Sutton's doubt, from the Dwarkesh podcast, that LLMs are "bitter lesson pilled", since they train on human and finite data, and Sutton's alternative: an agent that learns only from interaction through reinforcement learning, with intrinsic rewards and continual learning at test time (#1). He agrees that frontier LLMs carry human input at every stage and that no clean learn-from-experience algorithm exists, and he still thinks the bitter lesson is correct, as an ideal to pursue more than reach (#1). He disagrees that AlphaZero and animals show such an algorithm is practical: Go is a simple closed environment, and animal brains start from an initialization evolution encoded in DNA, so much of their "learning" is maturation or finetuning (#1). Pretraining, he says, is "our crappy evolution", one solution to the cold-start problem before RL finetuning (#1). Today's frontier research summons ghosts, statistical distillations of human documents, which may be finetuned toward animals or stay different, like planes and birds; he calls the pod good "real talk" for researchers stuck in exploit mode (#1).
