## Summary
He says that in December the chunks of code his agents produced started coming out fine, he trusted them more and more, and he has been coding side projects all the time since (@01:08, @01:39, @02:11). He restates Software 3.0 as prompting, with the context window as the lever over an LLM interpreter, and gives an agent-pasted installer and a one-prompt Gemini version of MenuGen as examples, the second of which makes his own app "spurious" (@03:18, @03:50, @05:59); he extrapolates, hedged, to neural nets as the host process with CPUs as coprocessors (@08:40, @09:13). He explains jaggedness as verifiability plus what the labs care about, with a car-wash question and chess data as examples (@09:47, @11:24, @12:00, @12:32). Adopting the host's term, he separates vibe coding, which raises the floor, from agentic engineering, which keeps professional software's quality bar while going faster, and says hiring, spec-writing and human taste have to follow (@15:49, @16:22, @18:34, @20:44, @21:51). He closes on agent-native infrastructure and a tweet he endorses, that thinking can be outsourced but understanding cannot (@25:41, @26:47, @27:55).
