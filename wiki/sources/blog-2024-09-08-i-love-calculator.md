# I love calculator
Raw: [[raw/blog/i-love-calculator]] · Original: https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/i-love-calculator/

The post has no headings, so all citations are `§intro`.

## Summary
Browsing a book on the history of the pocket calculator, he realises he loves the calculator, not as a device to buy today but as a product of technology and a symbol of a philosophy (§intro). He describes it as a plugin for the brain that extends its arithmetic, with an almost zero "dependency footprint": it needs only light or commodity batteries, is bought in one exchange and is then yours, and would work if carried thousands of years back in time (§intro). He contrasts it with modern technology through a long list of what it does not do: no internet setup, no accounts or logins, no location or bluetooth requests, no subscriptions or upsells, no usage tracking, no data harvesting or breaches, no breaking when servers go down; its computation stays private on the device (§intro). His example of the opposite is a CO2 monitor that demanded an account, an app and precise location before it would report his room's CO2 (§intro). He asks whether this drift is companies hyperoptimizing shareholder value under regulation, and says consumers and developers should remember what technology could be: like the calculator, at least as a platonic ideal to approach along various dimensions (§intro). He closes with links to related work by others that he found enjoyable and influential (§intro).
