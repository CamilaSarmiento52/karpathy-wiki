# Finding the Best Sleep Tracker
Raw: [[raw/blog/finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]] · Original: https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/finding-the-best-sleep-tracker/

The post has no headings; some paragraphs open with a label. Citations use `§intro (<label>)` for Qualitative assessment, Matters of Heart Rate, Yes, sleep matters and Shopping recommendations, and plain `§intro` for the paragraphs before the first label.

## Summary
After a Bryan Johnson video on sleep habits, which he says he came across about two months before writing, he resolved to improve his sleep and first to measure it, so he wore four trackers at once, Oura, Whoop, an 8Sleep Pod 4 Ultra and an Apple Watch with AutoSleep, and logged their numbers each morning in a published spreadsheet (§intro). After about two months he rates Oura and Whoop as "Tier 1", 8Sleep as ok, and Apple Watch with AutoSleep as really terrible, its scores nearly random (§intro (Qualitative assessment)). Score histograms show AutoSleep and 8Sleep saturating near 100, Whoop somewhat, and Oura with the most dynamic range; pairwise score correlations are highest for Whoop and Oura (0.65) and lowest for Whoop and AutoSleep (0.14) (§intro (Qualitative assessment)). Resting heart rate agrees closely across trackers, does not clearly correlate with sleep score, and, with his regular Zone 2 and HIIT exercise, fell slightly over the 60 days while HRV rose (§intro (Matters of Heart Rate)). He says with absolute certainty that his sleep scores correlate strongly with the quality of his work that day, and recommends Whoop for convenient tracking and Oura for the best tracking if the ring suits you (§intro (Yes, sleep matters), (Shopping recommendations)).
