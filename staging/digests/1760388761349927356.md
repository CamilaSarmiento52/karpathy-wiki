# on technical accessibility

## Summary
He recalls that when he first published the micrograd repo it got some traction and then stagnated, but once he made the video building it from scratch the repo went through hockey-stick growth and became an often-cited reference for learning backpropagation, though the code itself had not changed. He had thought the roughly 200-line, extensively commented code with a README was self-explanatory, and when people did not engage he brushed it off. He concludes the obstacle was accessibility: the video almost 100X'd interest in the same code, from beginners and also from expert friends he thinks were deterred by a barrier to entry. He argues technical people are biased toward putting up the final thing and assuming it explains itself, and may leave 10–100X of a work's potential on the table by not making it accessible. His summary: build the thing, then build the ramp.
