## Summary
He reports Richard Sutton's doubt, from the Dwarkesh podcast, that LLMs are "bitter lesson pilled", since they train on human and finite data, and Sutton's alternative: an agent that learns only from interaction through reinforcement learning, with intrinsic rewards and continual learning at test time. He agrees that frontier LLMs carry human input at every stage and that no clean learn-from-experience algorithm exists, and he still thinks the bitter lesson is correct, as an ideal to pursue more than reach. He disagrees that AlphaZero and animals show such an algorithm is practical: Go is a simple closed environment, and animal brains start from an initialization evolution encoded in DNA, so much of their "learning" is maturation or finetuning. Pretraining, he says, is "our crappy evolution", one solution to the cold-start problem before RL finetuning. Today's frontier research summons ghosts, statistical distillations of human documents, which may be finetuned toward animals or stay different, like planes and birds; he calls the pod good "real talk" for researchers stuck in exploit mode.
